## Directly Usable Theoretical Mechanisms

The corpus directly supports mechanisms around land overvaluation, wealth-capital measurement, credit/collateral amplification, wobbly dynamics, and debt rollover. These are usable as theoretical mechanisms, not as direct empirical claims about Latin America.

## Mechanisms Requiring Institutional Translation

Land-credit and real-estate collateral mechanisms require translation into local banking rules, tenure regimes, mortgage depth, informal housing markets, fiscal structures, and urban planning institutions.

## Claims Dependent on Advanced-Economy Assumptions

Claims grounded in advanced-economy wealth measurement, deep mortgage markets, or formal land markets must be treated as conditional. They cannot be transferred as-is to Latin American land-rent financialization cases.

## Claims Requiring Empirical Validation in Latin America

Any claim about magnitude, incidence, class distribution, urban land channels, or policy effects requires regional empirical validation. Page-specific theoretical support is not enough for empirical claims.

## Missing Links to Urban Land-Rent Financialization

The SHT corpus gives a strong theoretical reconstruction of land, credit, wealth, and dynamic inefficiency. It does not by itself supply a complete account of Latin American urban informality, zoning, municipal finance, peripheral expansion, dollarization, foreign capital, or social housing finance.
