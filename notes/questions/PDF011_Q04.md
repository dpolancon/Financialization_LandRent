## NotebookLM question

How are the new stylized facts of growth and distribution used to criticize standard capital-labor models?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The paper uses the new stylized facts of growth and distribution—specifically the simultaneous rise in the wealth-income ratio, the stagnation of average wages, the rising share of capital, and the persistent high return to capital—to demonstrate the empirical failure of standard neoclassical capital-labor models [1, 2]. Stiglitz argues that if the observed surge in aggregate wealth actually represented an accumulation of real productive capital, fundamental economic laws such as diminishing returns would dictate that the rate of return on capital must fall and the average wage must rise [3, 4]. However, the empirical data shows exactly the opposite: the capital share has increased and average wages have stagnated or declined [1, 5]. Furthermore, standard models predicting capital deepening with an elasticity of substitution less than unity mathematically require an increasing share of labor, which explicitly contradicts the new stylized facts [4, 6]. Because these neoclassical capital-labor models cannot reconcile these empirical contradictions, they are fundamentally inadequate for explaining modern macroeconomic trends unless one abandons the assumption that measured wealth equals productive capital and accounts for the explosive growth of non-produced assets and capitalized rents [7-9].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: The new stylized facts empirically contradict standard capital-labor models, which falsely predict that the observed massive increases in wealth (if interpreted as true productive capital) would force higher average wages, a lower return to capital, and a higher labor share.
- **Mechanism**:
  - New empirical data reveals rising wealth-income ratios, rising capital income shares, stagnant average wages, and steady returns to capital.
  - Standard neoclassical models equate wealth with productive capital and assume competitive markets.
  - In these models, a massive accumulation of capital relative to labor mathematically forces the marginal product of capital to fall and the average wage to rise due to diminishing returns.
  - Because the empirical reality completely contradicts these theoretical requirements, standard models are invalid for explaining current trends, proving that the observed wealth surge must consist of non-productive capitalized rents rather than real capital accumulation.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: The average wage derivative $\overline{w}^{\prime}(K)=-\frac{F_{KK}K}{L}>0$, demonstrating that in standard competitive models, capital deepening must unambiguously increase the average wage, contradicting recent empirical stagnation.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: critique, land overvaluation
- **Critical caveat**: Applying this critique of standard capital-labor models to Latin America requires external verification, as the paper relies entirely on advanced-economy stylized facts (e.g., US wage stagnation and wealth-income ratios) and does not model the region's specific structural dependencies, such as primary commodity exports, informality, and peripheral financial constraints.
