## NotebookLM question

What should the literature review say about using R versus G as a sufficient condition for rollover?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The literature review should explicitly critique the conventional reliance on the $R < G$ condition as a sufficient theoretical metric for evaluating infinite debt rollover, arguing that this heuristic breaks down once a non-reproducible asset like land is introduced into macroeconomic models. Standard landless models (e.g., Diamond, 1965) establish that infinite debt rollover is possible if and only if the safe interest rate is below the economic growth rate ($R < G$), a state representing dynamic inefficiency and oversaving [1, 2]. However, Hirano and Toda (2026) demonstrate that the mere existence of land fundamentally alters this paradigm. Under balanced growth, land mathematically forces $R > G$, which conventionally precludes Ponzi schemes [3, 4]. Yet, under unbalanced growth, if the natural interest rate is sufficiently low, a rational land bubble inevitably emerges to absorb excess savings, restoring Pareto efficiency [5]. Because this efficient bubble grows exactly at the rate of interest, the government can sustainably roll over infinite debt by issuing bonds to replace a portion of the bubble [6, 7]. Consequently, the paper dictates that the literature review must abandon the simplistic $G$ versus $R$ relationship derived from landless models, as debt rollover is perfectly feasible in a Pareto efficient land economy regardless of dynamic inefficiency [2, 4].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: The literature review must argue that $R < G$ is not a theoretically coherent or sufficient condition for infinite debt rollover, because introducing land demonstrates that sustainable debt rollover can occur in perfectly Pareto-efficient economies where dynamic inefficiency is absent.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Conventional macroeconomic models without land equate the feasibility of infinite debt rollover exclusively with the dynamic inefficiency of oversaving, defined by $R < G$.
  - In an economy with land under balanced growth, the asset's return structurally forces the interest rate above the growth rate ($R > G$), which conventionally makes a Ponzi scheme impossible.
  - However, under unbalanced growth with high savings demand, an efficient land bubble must inevitably emerge to clear the market.
  - The fiscal authority can issue public debt to absorb a proportional share of this growing, efficient bubble, rendering infinite debt rollover possible and completely severing the reliance on the $R < G$ dynamic inefficiency condition.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Theorem 1 and Corollary 2, which prove that an asymptotically bubbly, Pareto efficient equilibrium permits infinite debt rollover ($B_{t+1} = R_t B_t$).
- **Relevance for the project memo**: debt rollover, critique
- **Critical caveat**: Asserting the irrelevance of the $R < G$ heuristic for Latin American sovereign debt requires external verification, as the region's debt sustainability is frequently constrained by exogenous peripheral vulnerabilities—such as foreign currency denomination, "sudden stops" in capital flows, and sovereign risk premiums—rather than the closed-economy overlapping-generations mechanics modeled here.
