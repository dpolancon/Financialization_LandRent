## NotebookLM question

How does the paper revise McCallum’s claim about Pareto efficiency in land economies?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] Hirano and Toda (2026) revise McCallum’s (1987) influential claim that an overlapping generations economy with land is inherently Pareto efficient by demonstrating that his result relies on strict "knife-edge" mathematical assumptions [1-3]. McCallum correctly proved that land forces the interest rate above the economic growth rate ($R > G$) under steady-state balanced growth, but the authors show this implicitly restricts the production function to an elasticity of substitution exactly equal to one ($\sigma = 1$) [2, 4, 5]. By introducing more empirically plausible unbalanced growth conditions—where labor productivity outpaces land productivity ($G > 1$) and the elasticity of substitution between land and non-land factors strictly exceeds one ($\sigma > 1$)—the authors mathematically prove that land prices can become asymptotically negligible relative to aggregate endowments [2, 6]. Under these broader conditions, a Pareto inefficient equilibrium where the interest rate falls below the economic growth rate ($R < G$) can indeed emerge, definitively refuting McCallum's generalized claim [3, 6].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: The paper refutes McCallum’s generalized claim that land guarantees Pareto efficiency by proving his result depends on knife-edge balanced-growth restrictions, showing instead that Pareto inefficient equilibria can emerge under realistic unbalanced growth conditions.
- **Mechanism**:
  - McCallum assumed steady-state balanced growth, which mathematically forces the production function to exhibit an elasticity of substitution exactly equal to one ($\sigma = 1$).
  - Under this strict assumption, land prices and output grow at the exact same rate, guaranteeing that the interest rate strictly exceeds the growth rate ($R > G$) and thereby ensuring Pareto efficiency.
  - Hirano and Toda relax this restriction by analyzing unbalanced growth where labor productivity grows faster than land productivity ($G > 1$) and the inputs are highly substitutable ($\sigma > 1$).
  - In this environment, aggregate endowments outpace land rent growth, rendering the land asset asymptotically negligible; this allows the natural interest rate to fall below the growth rate ($R < G$), generating a Pareto inefficient equilibrium.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Proposition 3.4 (proving a Pareto inefficient equilibrium exists under unbalanced growth) and Proposition 4.1 (proving McCallum’s labor-share convergence necessitates a knife-edge Cobb-Douglas production function equivalent to $\sigma = 1$).
- **Relevance for the project memo**: critique
- **Critical caveat**: Applying this theoretical finding regarding Pareto inefficiency to Latin America requires external verification, as the paper relies on a stylized, closed-economy overlapping-generations model and completely abstracts from the region's peripheral macroeconomic realities, such as sovereign risk premiums, foreign capital dependence, and structural external debt limits.
