## NotebookLM question

What conventional McCallum/Homburg result about land, R>G, and debt rollover is being challenged?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The paper challenges the conventional McCallum (1987) and Homburg (1991) result that introducing land—a productive, non-reproducible, and non-depreciating asset—into an overlapping generations model inherently guarantees Pareto efficiency by mathematically forcing the safe interest rate to exceed the economic growth rate ($R>G$), which conventionally makes infinite debt rollover (a Ponzi scheme) impossible. Hirano and Toda demonstrate that this well-known "land efficiency" result relies on strict, "knife-edge" mathematical assumptions, specifically steady-state balanced growth which implicitly restricts the production function to an elasticity of substitution exactly equal to one [1-3]. By relaxing these restrictions and introducing empirically plausible unbalanced growth—where labor productivity grows faster than fixed land productivity and the elasticity of substitution strictly exceeds one ($\sigma>1$)—the authors mathematically prove that land prices can become asymptotically negligible relative to aggregate endowments. Under these broader conditions, a Pareto inefficient equilibrium where the interest rate falls below the growth rate ($R<G$) can indeed emerge, overturning the generalized McCallum/Homburg claim and restoring the possibility of infinite debt rollover even in an economy with land [2, 4, 5].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: The paper refutes the conventional McCallum/Homburg claim that land unconditionally guarantees Pareto efficiency ($R>G$), proving that their result depends on knife-edge balanced-growth restrictions and that infinite debt rollover remains possible under realistic unbalanced growth conditions.
- **Mechanism**:
  - McCallum and Homburg assumed steady-state balanced growth, where land rents and prices grow at the exact same rate as overall output.
  - This structural assumption forces the asset's total rate of return (and thus the safe interest rate) strictly above the economic growth rate ($R>G$), which inherently precludes sustainable debt rollover.
  - Hirano and Toda show this balanced-growth assumption implicitly requires a Cobb-Douglas production function where the elasticity of substitution between land and labor is exactly one ($\sigma=1$).
  - By introducing unbalanced growth where labor productivity outpaces land productivity ($G>1$) and inputs are highly substitutable ($\sigma>1$), land becomes asymptotically negligible relative to the overall economy; this allows the natural interest rate to fall below the growth rate ($R<G$), enabling Pareto inefficiency and sustainable infinite debt rollover.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Proposition 3.4 (proving a Pareto inefficient equilibrium exists under unbalanced growth) and Proposition 4.1 (proving McCallum’s labor-share convergence necessitates a knife-edge production function equivalent to $\sigma=1$).
- **Relevance for the project memo**: critique, debt rollover
- **Critical caveat**: Applying this theoretical critique of the McCallum/Homburg result to Latin American sovereign debt requires external verification, as the paper's framework is a deterministic, closed-economy overlapping-generations model that completely abstracts from the region's exposure to open-economy capital flight, dollarization, and high sovereign risk premiums.
