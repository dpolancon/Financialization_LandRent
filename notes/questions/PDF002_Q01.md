## NotebookLM question

What conventional McCallum/Homburg result about land, R>G, and debt rollover is being challenged?

## NotebookLM answer

> [DRAFT_BY_QWEN] The paper challenges the conventional view (associated with McCallum and Homburg) that in a dynamically efficient economy where the interest rate exceeds the growth rate ($R > G$), government debt cannot be rolled over indefinitely without a primary surplus, and land bubbles cannot exist. The authors argue that with land as a productive asset and unbalanced growth, infinite debt rollover can be sustainable even when $R > G$ on average, because land's price appreciation can offset the interest burden, or because the effective return on land differs from the risk-free rate in a way that supports rollover. [NEEDS_VERIFICATION]

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Infinite debt rollover is possible even in dynamically efficient economies ($R > G$) when land is present and growth is unbalanced.
- **Mechanism**: 
  - Conventional theory: $R > G$ implies debt is unsustainable without surpluses.
  - Land introduces a collateral/wealth effect that alters the dynamics.
  - Unbalanced growth allows land prices to appreciate sufficiently.
  - This creates a "Ponzi-like" sustainability for debt backed by land.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Challenge to the $R > G$ sustainability condition in OLG models with land.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: Directly addresses the "land speculation & debt rollover" project question.
- **Critical caveat**: Latin American sovereign debt dynamics may be constrained by external financing limits and currency mismatches, not captured in closed-economy land models.
