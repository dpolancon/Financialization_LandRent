## NotebookLM question

What policy interventions are analyzed and what are their welfare effects?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The paper analyzes several fiscal policy interventions—specifically land taxes, capital gains taxes, capital subsidies, and capital taxes—demonstrating that while some interventions reduce crowding out, they can paradoxically increase macroeconomic volatility unless comprehensively designed [1-3]. A tax on land rents or capital gains increases steady-state capital but, surprisingly, amplifies the range and pace of land price fluctuations during expectation-driven wobbly dynamics [2, 3]. In contrast, applying a capital subsidy during a capital surplus regime or a capital tax during a capital shortage regime reduces the magnitude of dynamic inefficiency and crowding out, thereby increasing aggregate consumption and overall social welfare under an egalitarian welfare function [4-6]. Ultimately, the authors prove that a credible government commitment to a coordinated capital income and wage tax can eliminate wobbly dynamics entirely, achieving a unique, dynamically efficient steady state at full employment [7, 8].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: The paper demonstrates that while standard land and capital gains taxes can paradoxically increase macroeconomic volatility, targeted capital taxes/subsidies and credible full-commitment tax regimes can successfully eliminate wobbly dynamics, reduce capital crowding-out, and maximize social welfare.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Standard land or capital gains taxes reduce the fundamental dividend value of land, which paradoxically requires land prices to rise even faster during booms to satisfy the rational-expectations capital arbitrage equation, amplifying overall price volatility.
  - Alternatively, imposing a capital tax during a capital shortage regime (using proceeds for transfers to the young) lowers the return to capital, which slows the rate of land price increases.
  - This slower asset-price growth reduces the speculative crowding-out effect, allowing more domestic savings to flow into productive capital accumulation, raising output and aggregate consumption.
  - Furthermore, if the government credibly commits to guaranteeing a specific full-employment interest rate via taxes, it collapses the multiplicity of equilibria into a single, dynamically efficient full-employment state.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Proposition 5: With credible commitment to a capital income tax and a wage tax, the government can achieve just full employment $k^f$ as the unique rational expectations equilibrium, and the equilibrium is dynamically efficient.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: credit crowding-out
- **Critical caveat**: Evaluating these specific fiscal policy interventions for Latin American economies requires external verification, as the optimal tax mechanisms assume a closed economy with perfect institutional credibility, abstracting from the region's capital flight risks, foreign debt constraints, and dollarized real estate dynamics.
