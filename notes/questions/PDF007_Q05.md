## NotebookLM question

How does land crowd out productive capital and worsen macro instability?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] Land crowds out productive capital because it serves as a competing, non-produced store of value that absorbs finite domestic life-cycle savings [1, 2]. Mathematically, as land prices ($P_t$) rise, they mechanically subtract from the savings available for real capital accumulation ($k_{t+1}$) [2]. This crowding-out worsens macroeconomic instability by triggering a vicious feedback loop: lower capital investment depresses wages and raises the interest rate, which in turn forces land prices to increase even faster to satisfy capital arbitrage [3]. Consequently, the introduction of land expands the mathematical parameter space for "wobbly dynamics," generating endogenous phase transitions and expectation-driven boom-bust cycles—and even episodic involuntary unemployment—in economies that would otherwise converge to a stable, unique steady state [4-6].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Land acts as a non-produced asset that absorbs finite domestic savings, directly displacing productive capital investment and structurally expanding the mathematical conditions under which the economy suffers from expectation-driven instability and boom-bust cycles.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Young agents allocate their finite life-cycle savings between purchasing productive capital and non-produced land.
  - Rising land prices absorb a larger share of these savings, mechanically crowding out real capital accumulation.
  - Lower capital investment depresses wages and increases the interest rate, which forces land prices to rise even faster to satisfy capital arbitrage.
  - This feedback loop expands the range of multiple momentary equilibria, causing the economy to endogenously transition between unsustainable, expectation-driven booms and crashes.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: The savings/capital accumulation equation (2), $k_{t+1} + P_t = s_t(w_t + e)$, demonstrating that higher land prices $P_t$ directly reduce capital investment $k_{t+1}$.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: credit crowding-out, critique
- **Critical caveat**: Applying this mechanism to Latin America requires external verification, as the model structurally assumes a closed economy where land speculation is financed strictly by domestic life-cycle savings, abstracting from the region's exposure to external debt and foreign capital flows.
