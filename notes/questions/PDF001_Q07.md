## NotebookLM question

What are the limitations of treating land overvaluation through substitution elasticity and technological progress for Latin American land-rent financialization?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The paper models land overvaluation strictly as a structural consequence of unbalanced growth (where non-land technological progress outpaces land productivity) combined with an elasticity of substitution strictly greater than one ($\sigma > 1$). However, applying this framework to Latin American land-rent financialization requires external verification because the authors explicitly construct a "bare-bones model" that abstracts entirely from the role of land as collateral and the existence of credit constraints [1]. By isolating the cause of overvaluation to real-economy technological differentials and the demographic savings motives of workers, the model omits the financial channels—such as mortgage credit expansions, speculative leverage, dollarized real estate markets, and international capital flows—that are typically central to the financialization of land in peripheral economies. Consequently, the primary limitation of this approach is that it cannot analyze the credit-driven boom-bust cycles characteristic of modern financialized land regimes.

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Treating land overvaluation purely through the lens of substitution elasticity and unbalanced technological progress is limited because the theoretical framework explicitly abstracts from credit markets, leverage, and the role of land as collateral.
- **Mechanism**:
  - The model derives overvaluation strictly from unbalanced growth, where technological progress in labor outpaces fixed land productivity.
  - Because the elasticity of substitution is greater than one ($\sigma > 1$), fundamental land rents grow slower than aggregate wages.
  - The persistent influx of growing wage savings directed at land mathematically forces overvaluation.
  - However, because the model explicitly abstracts from collateral and credit, it mathematically ignores the leverage dynamics and financial flows that drive actual land-rent financialization.

- **Relevance for the project memo**: critique, land overvaluation
- **Critical caveat**: Analyzing Latin American land-rent financialization using this model requires external verification, as the paper deliberately abstracts from the credit constraints, sovereign risk, and foreign-capital dependencies that heavily define the region's financialized real estate markets.
