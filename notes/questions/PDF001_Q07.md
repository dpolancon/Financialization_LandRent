## NotebookLM question

What are the limitations of treating land overvaluation through substitution elasticity and technological progress for Latin American land-rent financialization?

## NotebookLM answer

> [DRAFT_BY_QWEN] The model's reliance on $\sigma > 1$ and balanced/unbalanced growth paths assumes well-functioning markets and clear property rights. In Latin America, land markets are often characterized by informality, weak enforcement of property rights, and significant state intervention. Additionally, the "technological progress" assumption may not capture the reality of commodity-dependent economies where land value is tied to global prices rather than domestic productivity. The model also abstracts from political economy factors like land grabbing and elite capture, which are central to Latin American land dynamics. [NEEDS_VERIFICATION]

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: The theoretical framework's assumptions (elasticity, growth paths) may not hold in Latin America due to informality, weak institutions, and commodity dependence.
- **Mechanism**: 
  - Informal markets distort price signals and collateral value.
  - Commodity cycles drive land rents more than domestic tech progress.
  - Political economy factors (elite capture) are absent from the model.
  - $\sigma$ is likely heterogeneous and context-dependent.
