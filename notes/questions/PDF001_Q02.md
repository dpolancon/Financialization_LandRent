## NotebookLM question

What are the formal conditions behind the Land Overvaluation Theorem, especially the role of unbalanced productivity growth and elasticity of substitution above one?

## NotebookLM answer

> [DRAFT_BY_QWEN] The Land Overvaluation Theorem holds when two key conditions are met: (1) the economy experiences unbalanced productivity growth, where capital-augmenting technological progress outpaces land-augmenting progress, and (2) the elasticity of substitution between land and other inputs (capital/labor) in production is strictly greater than one ($\sigma > 1$). Under these conditions, the relative price of land rises sufficiently fast to sustain equilibrium prices that exceed the fundamental value (discounted rental stream), even as land's productive share declines. [NEEDS_VERIFICATION]

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Land overvaluation is structurally sustained when unbalanced growth combines with an elasticity of substitution greater than one, allowing land prices to decouple from productive fundamentals.
- **Mechanism**: 
  - Unbalanced growth reduces land's marginal product relative to capital.
  - With $\sigma > 1$, land and capital are gross substitutes; demand shifts maintain land's value.
  - Relative land prices rise faster than the decline in productive contribution.
  - Equilibrium prices exceed fundamental discounted rents, creating a persistent overvaluation wedge.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Land Overvaluation Theorem: Requires $\sigma > 1$ and unbalanced growth path for $P_t > \sum \beta^s R_{t+s}$.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: Provides the mathematical foundation for the "Land Overvaluation Theorem" and identifies $\sigma$ as a critical calibration parameter.
- **Critical caveat**: Estimating $\sigma$ for Latin American land markets is difficult due to structural rigidities, informality, and heterogeneous land quality, which may violate the $\sigma > 1$ assumption.
