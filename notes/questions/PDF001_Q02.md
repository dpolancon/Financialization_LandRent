## NotebookLM question

What are the formal conditions behind the Land Overvaluation Theorem, especially the role of unbalanced productivity growth and elasticity of substitution above one?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The formal conditions behind the Land Overvaluation Theorem (Theorem 1) require a neoclassical production function where the elasticity of substitution between land and non-land factors strictly exceeds one ($\sigma > 1$ at high input levels) combined with long-run unbalanced growth where non-land (labor) productivity outpaces land productivity. Formally, the theorem proves that land becomes inevitably overvalued if the expected infinite sum of the ratio of labor to land productivity, raised to the power of $1/\sigma - 1$, is finite: $E_{0}\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(A_{Ht}/A_{Xt})^{1/\sigma-1}<\infty$ (Hirano & Toda, 2025). Because the elasticity of substitution is greater than one, the exponent $(1/\sigma - 1)$ is strictly negative. When the economy experiences unbalanced growth, labor productivity ($A_{Ht}$) grows faster than land productivity ($A_{Xt}$), meaning their ratio goes to infinity over time; raised to a negative exponent, this ensures the mathematical series converges. Economically, this condition dictates that while young agents' wages and savings grow rapidly alongside labor productivity, the high substitutability of inputs suppresses the growth of fundamental land rents. Consequently, the surging influx of aggregate savings directed at fixed land bids up land prices at a structurally faster rate than its underlying dividends can grow, permanently decoupling the asset's price from its fundamental rent.

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: The Land Overvaluation Theorem formally proves that land inevitably becomes overvalued if the elasticity of substitution between land and labor strictly exceeds one ($\sigma > 1$) and labor productivity structurally outpaces land productivity.
- **Mechanism**:
  - The economy undergoes unbalanced growth where technological progress in labor/human capital ($A_{Ht}$) grows faster than productivity in fixed land ($A_{Xt}$), driving rapid growth in aggregate wages.
  - Because the elasticity of substitution between land and labor is greater than one ($\sigma > 1$), the growth of fundamental land rents is mathematically suppressed relative to aggregate economic output.
  - Young workers save a fraction of their rapidly growing labor income by purchasing durable land as a store of value.
  - This persistent influx of growing savings bids up the price of land much faster than its underlying rental yield can grow, mathematically forcing an upward trend in the price-rent ratio and inevitable land overvaluation.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Theorem 1 (Land Overvaluation Theorem), which proves land overvaluation emerges if $\sigma > 1$ and $E_{0}\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(A_{Ht}/A_{Xt})^{1/\sigma-1}<\infty$.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: land overvaluation
- **Critical caveat**: Applying these precise formal conditions to Latin America requires external verification, as the theoretical model abstracts entirely from the region's distinct structural realities, such as dollarized real estate sectors, commodity-export-driven land yields, and deeply informal urban housing markets.
