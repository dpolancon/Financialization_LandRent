## NotebookLM question

How can Pareto efficiency coexist with infinite debt rollover in the model?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] In the model, Pareto efficiency coexists with infinite debt rollover because the presence of land fundamentally alters the economic conditions required to sustain a Ponzi scheme [1, 2]. When an economy with land experiences a sufficiently low natural interest rate environment—often driven by high savings demand from young workers under unbalanced growth—the fundamental value of land would mathematically explode to infinity without a bubble [3]. To prevent this and clear the market, an efficient land price bubble must inevitably emerge [1, 3]. Because this asymptotically bubbly equilibrium successfully achieves Pareto efficiency, and the bubble component naturally grows at the safe rate of interest, the fiscal authority can safely divide the bubble, substituting a proportional share of it with public debt [4]. Consequently, the government can sustain deficits indefinitely by rolling over this debt [4]. Infinite debt rollover thus becomes feasible precisely because of this inevitable, efficient land bubble, rendering the conventional requirement of dynamic inefficiency (Pareto inefficiency) completely irrelevant [2].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Infinite debt rollover is possible in a Pareto efficient land economy because the fiscal authority can sustainably issue public debt to absorb a proportional share of an inevitably emerging, efficient land price bubble.
- **Mechanism**:
  - High savings motives suppress the natural interest rate, which would ordinarily cause fundamental land prices to become infinite.
  - To maintain equilibrium, an asymptotically growing land price bubble inevitably emerges to absorb the excess savings, achieving Pareto efficiency.
  - Within this efficient equilibrium, the bubble component of the land asset mathematically grows at the rate of interest.
  - The fiscal authority can therefore construct an observationally equivalent equilibrium by arbitrarily dividing this efficient bubble and issuing public debt to replace a portion of it, enabling sustainable infinite debt rollover.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Theorem 1 (proving all equilibria are asymptotically bubbly and Pareto efficient) and Corollary 2 (proving infinite debt rollover is possible by defining public debt as $B_t = \theta(P_t - V_t)$ where $B_{t+1} = R_t B_t$).
- **Relevance for the project memo**: debt rollover
- **Critical caveat**: Applying this closed-economy, deterministic mechanism for debt rollover to Latin America requires external verification, as the model entirely abstracts from the region's exposure to aggregate uncertainty, sovereign risk premiums, capital flight, and foreign-currency-denominated public debt.
