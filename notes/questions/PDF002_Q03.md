## NotebookLM question

How can Pareto efficiency coexist with infinite debt rollover in the model?

## NotebookLM answer

> [DRAFT_BY_QWEN] The paper demonstrates that infinite debt rollover (a Ponzi scheme) can be Pareto efficient in an OLG economy with land if the economy is dynamically inefficient ($R < G$) or if land overvaluation absorbs excess savings. In standard models, Ponzi schemes are inefficient transfers, but here, the presence of land and unbalanced growth alters the intertemporal budget constraints. The rollover effectively acts as a mechanism to transfer resources across generations in a way that improves welfare, provided the land asset's value grows sufficiently to sustain the debt without crowding out productive investment. [NEEDS_VERIFICATION]

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Infinite debt rollover can be Pareto efficient when land acts as a store of value that absorbs excess savings and facilitates intergenerational transfers.
- **Mechanism**: 
  - In dynamically inefficient economies, capital accumulation is excessive.
  - Debt rollover absorbs savings that would otherwise go into unproductive capital.
  - Land's appreciation supports the debt service.
  - Welfare improves as resources are reallocated efficiently across generations.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Pareto efficiency condition for Ponzi schemes in OLG with land.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: Challenges the view that debt rollover is inherently unsustainable or inefficient.
- **Critical caveat**: Latin American economies often face external constraints and capital flight, which may prevent the "efficient" rollover assumed in closed models.
