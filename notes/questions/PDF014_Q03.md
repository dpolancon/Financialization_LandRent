## NotebookLM question

Why does balanced growth in an economy with land tend to preclude debt rollover?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] In an economy with land, balanced growth mathematically forces the safe interest rate to exceed the economic growth rate ($R > G$), which inherently precludes infinite debt rollover [1, 2]. Under a balanced growth path, the price and rent of a productive, non-reproducible asset like land grow at the exact same rate as aggregate output [1]. Because land yields a positive dividend (rent) in addition to this capital appreciation, its total rate of return must strictly exceed the economic growth rate [1, 3]. Due to the standard no-arbitrage condition, the rate of return on government bonds must equal the rate of return on holding land, establishing that $R > G$ is structurally guaranteed [2]. If infinite debt rollover were attempted in this environment, public debt would accumulate at the interest rate $R$; because $R > G$, the debt would grow faster than the economy itself, which is unsustainable and makes such Ponzi schemes impossible [4].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Balanced growth in a land economy guarantees that the interest rate strictly exceeds the economic growth rate ($R > G$), rendering infinite debt rollover impossible because public debt would unsustainably outpace economic growth.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Under a balanced growth path, both land rents and land prices grow at the exact same rate as aggregate economic output.
  - Because land provides a positive rent alongside this capital appreciation, its total rate of return strictly exceeds the growth rate of output.
  - The no-arbitrage condition forces the safe interest rate on government debt to match this high rate of return on land, mathematically guaranteeing $R > G$.
  - Consequently, any attempt at infinite debt rollover fails because the debt (growing at rate $R$) would grow faster than the economy (growing at rate $G$), making the scheme unsustainable.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Proposition 3.3, which proves that under balanced growth parameters ($G=1$ or $\sigma=1$), $R > G$ naturally emerges, Pareto efficiency is achieved, and infinite debt rollover is impossible.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: debt rollover, critique
- **Critical caveat**: Applying this balanced-growth impossibility theorem to Latin America requires external verification, as the region's economies are frequently characterized by structural volatility, external shocks, and unbalanced growth dynamics that deviate significantly from the steady-state assumptions of this model.
