## NotebookLM question

How does the model resolve the finite-land-price puzzle when the safe rate is below output growth?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The model resolves the finite-land-price puzzle—the theoretical conundrum where standard macroeconomic models predict infinite land prices if the safe interest rate falls below the economic growth rate—by introducing credit frictions and limited market participation [1, 2]. In this framework, non-entrepreneurial workers (savers) do not participate in the real estate market, leaving land to be priced exclusively by credit-constrained entrepreneurs [3, 4]. Because these entrepreneurs rely on leverage and continuously arbitrage their investments between productive capital and land, the effective discount rate used to capitalize future land rents is not the low safe interest rate ($r$), but rather the much higher leveraged rate of return derived from capital [4, 5]. Consequently, even when the safe interest rate is structurally lower than the economic growth rate ($1+g^* > 1+r^*$), the entrepreneurs' required leveraged return remains strictly higher than the growth rate [6]. This mathematically ensures that the present discounted value of land—and thus its equilibrium market price—remains finite [4, 7].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: The finite-land-price puzzle is resolved by assuming limited real estate market participation, which forces land to be discounted at the entrepreneurs' high leveraged rate of return on capital rather than the low safe interest rate.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Standard models assume universal market participation, meaning land is discounted by the safe interest rate; if this rate falls below the economic growth rate, the geometric series explodes and land prices become infinite.
  - This model restricts land ownership to entrepreneurs who face binding borrowing constraints (credit frictions).
  - Because entrepreneurs arbitrage their borrowed funds between land and capital, land must offer a leveraged rate of return equal to the high returns of productive capital.
  - This high leveraged return replaces the safe interest rate as the effective discount rate for land; because it strictly exceeds the economic growth rate, the present value of land remains perfectly finite even when the safe rate is extremely low.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Proposition 10, demonstrating that $R^c > 1+g^* > 1+r^*$, and the effective land discount rate $\frac{\lambda}{1-\theta^x+\frac{\theta^x\lambda}{1+r^*}}$, which replaces the standard $1+r^*$.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: critique, land overvaluation
- **Critical caveat**: Applying this specific resolution to Latin America requires external verification, as the mechanism relies on the stylized assumption of limited real estate participation and perfectly formalized arbitrage between capital and land, abstracting from the region's widespread informal land ownership, dollarized property markets, and high risk premiums.
