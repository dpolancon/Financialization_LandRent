## NotebookLM question

What is wobbly macro-dynamics and how does it differ from smooth convergence in representative-agent or Solow/RBC models?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] Wobbly macro-dynamics refers to a macroeconomic trajectory where the economy bounces around infinitely without converging to a steady state and without regular periodicity, all while remaining perfectly consistent with rational expectations [1, 2]. This starkly contrasts with standard Real Business Cycle (RBC), Solow, or representative-agent models, which strictly feature a unique momentary equilibrium and a single smooth, convergent path to a well-defined long-run steady state [3]. The divergence stems from the overlapping generations framework, where finite-lived, heterogeneous agents can exhibit savings rates that decrease as interest rates rise [4]. Because of this non-monotonic savings behavior, the economy can generate multiple momentary equilibria for a given capital stock, meaning that macroeconomic outcomes are driven dynamically by self-fulfilling beliefs or sentiments (bullish or bearish expectations) rather than converging deterministically to a unique point [1, 4].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Wobbly macro-dynamics describes a rational-expectations economy that infinitely fluctuates without converging or displaying regular periodicity, fundamentally challenging standard RBC and Solow models that predict smooth convergence to a unique steady state.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Standard models (RBC/Solow) assume infinitely lived representative agents, generating a unique momentary equilibrium and deterministic, smooth convergence.
  - In an overlapping generations model, heterogeneous, finite-lived agents may reduce their savings when interest rates increase (i.e., the income effect dominates the substitution effect).
  - This non-monotonic savings behavior creates multiple valid savings and investment levels (multiple momentary equilibria) for a given level of capital stock.
  - Consequently, self-fulfilling beliefs (bullish vs. bearish expectations) drive the economy to endlessly jump between high-investment and low-investment states without ever converging to a steady state.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Equation (A) $\Omega(k_{t+1}) = W(k_{t})$, defining the multiplicity of momentary equilibria, and Lemma 3, providing the sufficient conditions for $\Omega^{\prime}(k_{t+1}) < 0$ (which generates the non-monotonicity).
- **Relevance for the project memo**: critique
- **Critical caveat**: The authors explicitly state this is an exercise in pure theory modeling a closed economy without money, credit, or real estate; applying this purely domestic, expectation-driven volatility framework to Latin American business cycles requires external verification regarding the region's specific vulnerabilities to exogenous global financial shocks and foreign capital flows.
