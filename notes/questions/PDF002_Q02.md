## NotebookLM question

Under what conditions do land bubbles necessarily emerge in the OLG land economy?

## NotebookLM answer

> [DRAFT_BY_QWEN] In the OLG land economy, land bubbles necessarily emerge when the economy is dynamically inefficient ($R < G$) or when unbalanced growth creates a wedge between the return on land and the growth rate. Specifically, if the elasticity of substitution is high enough and productivity growth is unbalanced, the fundamental value of land may be finite while the equilibrium price diverges, or the price may sustain a bubble component that is essential for equilibrium existence. The paper likely shows that under certain parameterizations, a bubble is not just possible but necessary to clear the asset market. [NEEDS_VERIFICATION]

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Land bubbles are not anomalies but necessary equilibrium features under specific growth and substitution conditions.
- **Mechanism**: 
  - Dynamic inefficiency ($R < G$) typically supports bubbles.
  - Unbalanced growth can induce bubble-like dynamics even if $R > G$ on average.
  - High elasticity of substitution allows land prices to detach from rents.
  - Market clearing requires a bubble component to absorb savings.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Existence theorem for necessary land bubbles in OLG with unbalanced growth.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: Explains why land price inflation might be structural rather than cyclical.
- **Critical caveat**: In Latin America, "bubbles" may be conflated with speculative manias driven by external capital flows, which differ from the endogenous OLG mechanism.
