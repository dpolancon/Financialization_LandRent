## NotebookLM question

Under what conditions do land bubbles necessarily emerge in the OLG land economy?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] Land bubbles necessarily emerge in an overlapping generations (OLG) land economy when the natural interest rate is structurally suppressed below the asymptotic growth rate of land rents, which is itself strictly lower than the overall economic growth rate ($R < G_r < G$) [1]. This condition typically arises in economies experiencing unbalanced growth—where labor productivity outpaces fixed land productivity ($G > 1$) and the elasticity of substitution between the two exceeds one ($\sigma > 1$)—coupled with demographic factors such as a low relative income for the old generation [2, 3]. Because the old have low income, the young are forced to save aggressively to smooth their consumption, pushing the natural interest rate down [3]. If a land bubble did not form under these conditions, the interest rate would fall to this low natural level ($R < G_r$), mathematically causing the fundamental value of land (the discounted present value of its future dividends) to explode to infinity, which is an equilibrium impossibility [4]. Therefore, an asymptotically growing land price bubble inevitably emerges to absorb these excess savings, ensuring finite asset pricing and simultaneously achieving Pareto efficiency [1, 4].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Land bubbles inevitably emerge when high savings motives and unbalanced growth drive the natural interest rate below the growth rate of land dividends ($R < G_r < G$), preventing fundamental asset values from reaching infinity.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Unbalanced technological growth ($G > 1, \sigma > 1$) causes overall economic output to grow faster than fundamental land rents ($G_r < G$).
  - A low relative income for the old generation compels young workers to aggressively save to smooth their life-cycle consumption.
  - This influx of aggregate savings structurally suppresses the natural interest rate below the dividend growth rate ($R < G_r$).
  - To clear the market without fundamental land prices becoming mathematically infinite, an efficient land bubble must inevitably emerge to absorb the excess savings.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Theorem 1 ($R < G_r < G$) and Proposition 3.1 ($R < G^{1/\sigma}$ under CES production), which formally establish the conditions for the inevitable emergence of asymptotically bubbly equilibria.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: land overvaluation, debt rollover
- **Critical caveat**: Applying these precise demographic and unbalanced-growth conditions to Latin America requires external verification, as the framework relies on a deterministic, closed-economy OLG model and completely abstracts from the region's peripheral realities, such as dollarized urban real estate, sovereign risk premiums, and vulnerability to sudden stops in foreign capital.
