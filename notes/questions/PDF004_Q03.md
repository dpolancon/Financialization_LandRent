## NotebookLM question

How does land increase the scope for fluctuations relative to the baseline OLG model?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The introduction of land into the baseline OLG model significantly increases the scope for endogenous macroeconomic fluctuations by adding a non-produced alternative store of value that competes with productive capital. In the mathematical framework, as the price of land ($P_t$) increases, the savings-investment correspondence function ($\Omega$) shifts upward unevenly—specifically shifting up more for low values of future capital than for high values. This disproportionate shift expands the range of initial capital ($k_t$) for which multiple momentary equilibria can exist, meaning the economy can bounce between "bullish" and "bearish" states across a wider set of conditions than in the landless baseline. Consequently, land introduces a richer set of wobbly dynamics, where speculative real estate booms and their subsequent endogenous crashes increase the potential variability in economic activity, and under certain parameter configurations, wobbly dynamics become the only possible rational expectations trajectories.

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Introducing land as a competing store of value expands the mathematical bounds for multiple momentary equilibria, generating a richer set of endogenous fluctuations and increasing overall macroeconomic variability.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Land is introduced as an alternative asset, meaning finite savings must be split between productive capital investment and land purchases.
  - As speculative land prices rise, the savings-investment curve ($\Omega$) shifts upward unevenly, rising more for low values of future capital ($k_{t+1}$) than for high values.
  - This uneven shift expands the range of current capital ($k_t$) that can sustain multiple momentary equilibria, making "wobbly" expectation-driven switches possible across a broader set of parameters.
  - The resulting rational land booms and their inevitable bursting create severe boom-bust cycles, directly increasing the scope for episodic unemployment and dynamic inefficiency.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: The modified savings/capital accumulation equation, $\Omega(k_{t+1}, P_t) \equiv \frac{k_{t+1}}{s_t(k_{t+1})} + \frac{P_t}{s_t(k_{t+1})} = w(k_t) + e$, demonstrating that the range of $k_t$ for multiple equilibria increases as $P_t$ rises.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: critique
- **Critical caveat**: The framework’s generation of these expanded fluctuations relies strictly on a closed OLG economy driven entirely by domestic life-cycle savings. Applying this mechanism to Latin American macroeconomic volatility requires external verification regarding the region's exposure to exogenous global financial shocks and foreign capital flows.
