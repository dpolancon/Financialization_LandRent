## NotebookLM question

What are the key differences between small-open-economy and closed-economy versions?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The key difference between the two versions of the model is that the small-open-economy version assumes an exogenously fixed interest rate where workers lend savings either to entrepreneurs or abroad [1], whereas the closed-economy version introduces fiat money and determines the interest rate endogenously [1-3]. In the closed-economy model, workers hold fiat money as a store of value, allowing the authors to analyze how domestic monetary policy (the money growth rate) affects the macroeconomy [4-6]. The most crucial theoretical divergence occurs in how the economy responds to real estate shocks or financial liberalization: when the interest rate is endogenously determined in the closed economy, an increase in real estate productivity or collateral value simultaneously pushes the endogenous interest rate down [7]. This endogenous drop in borrowing costs further fuels leveraged land speculation, thereby significantly strengthening the "crowding-out" effect on productive capital compared to the small-open economy where the interest rate remains constant [7].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: The small-open economy relies on a fixed exogenous interest rate, whereas the closed economy endogenizes the interest rate through fiat money, which fundamentally amplifies the crowding-out effect of land speculation and enables the analysis of monetary policy.
- **Mechanism**:
  - In the small-open economy, non-entrepreneurial workers lend their savings at a fixed global interest rate, isolating borrowing costs from domestic real estate dynamics.
  - In the closed economy, fiat money acts as the alternative store of value, making the interest rate endogenous and directly tied to domestic savings and money supply growth.
  - When real estate collateral limits are relaxed or real estate productivity rises in the closed economy, it triggers an endogenous decline in the interest rate.
  - This endogenous rate reduction makes leverage cheaper, disproportionately encouraging further land speculation, which strengthens the crowding-out of productive capital investment compared to the fixed-rate open economy.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: The endogenous interest rate condition $1+r^* = \frac{1+g^*}{1+\mu}$, which links the interest rate to the money growth rate $\mu$, and the theoretical result that $\frac{d(1+g^*)}{d\theta^x} < 0$ is amplified because the derivative includes the endogenous fall in $r$.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: credit crowding-out, land-price inflation
- **Critical caveat**: Applying this clean theoretical distinction between small-open and closed economies to Latin America requires external verification, as the model abstracts from the region's specific semi-open realities, such as heavily dollarized domestic real estate markets, extreme vulnerability to foreign capital "sudden stops," and external sovereign debt constraints.
