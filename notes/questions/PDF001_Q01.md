## NotebookLM question

What problem does the paper call the disconnection between land's declining productive role and land's continuing role as a store of value?

## NotebookLM answer

> [DRAFT_BY_QWEN] The paper frames this disconnection as the structural root of "land overvaluation." As economies mature, land's contribution to aggregate production shrinks, yet financial systems continue to treat it as prime collateral and a safe store of value. This mismatch allows credit expansion and speculative demand to sustain land prices far above their fundamental rental yields, creating a persistent wedge that fuels financial fragility. [NEEDS_VERIFICATION]

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Land prices structurally decouple from productive fundamentals as land transitions from a production factor to a financialized collateral asset, enabling persistent overvaluation.
- **Mechanism**: 
  - Productive share of land declines with economic development.
  - Financial intermediaries maintain high collateral valuation for land.
  - Credit expansion amplifies demand for land as a wealth store.
  - Prices detach from yields, sustained by leverage and rollover expectations.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Land Overvaluation Theorem (intuition: collateral constraints permit equilibrium prices exceeding fundamental discounted rents).
- **Relevance for the project memo**: Directly underpins the "Land Overvaluation Theorem" and clarifies how credit expansion drives land-price inflation independent of real productivity.
- **Critical caveat**: Latin American land markets feature high informality, weaker property rights, and constrained mortgage markets, which may limit collateral-driven leverage and alter the overvaluation trajectory.
