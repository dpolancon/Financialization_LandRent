## NotebookLM question

Why does the paper argue that unbalanced growth is necessary for debt rollover with land?

## NotebookLM answer

> [DRAFT_BY_QWEN] The paper argues that under balanced growth, the interest rate $R$ and growth rate $G$ are constant, and if $R > G$, debt rollover is unsustainable. However, with unbalanced growth (e.g., land productivity grows slower than capital), the relative price of land rises over time. This appreciation allows land to serve as a better store of value and collateral, effectively lowering the effective interest burden on debt or increasing the collateral value to support rollover. Unbalanced growth creates the dynamic wedge necessary for the debt-to-GDP ratio to stabilize or for the rollover to be sustained by asset appreciation rather than primary surpluses. [NEEDS_VERIFICATION]

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Unbalanced growth is the key mechanism that allows land-backed debt rollover to persist by driving land price appreciation.
- **Mechanism**: 
  - Balanced growth implies constant $R$ and $G$; if $R > G$, rollover fails.
  - Unbalanced growth causes land's relative price to rise.
  - Rising land prices increase collateral value and wealth effects.
  - This supports debt service without requiring primary surpluses.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Dynamic path analysis showing $P_t$ growth under unbalanced productivity.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: Explains the structural conditions under which land financialization supports sovereign or private debt sustainability.
- **Critical caveat**: Latin American growth is often volatile and commodity-driven, not necessarily following the smooth unbalanced paths assumed in the model.
