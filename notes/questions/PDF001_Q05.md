## NotebookLM question

How does the urban-land application extend the general theorem?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The urban-land application extends the general Land Overvaluation Theorem by demonstrating that the theorem's mathematical conclusions hold even when land is not used directly to produce final consumption goods, but rather functions as an input for an intermediate "real estate" sector. Hirano and Toda (2025) formulate an urban economy where construction workers and land produce real estate, which is subsequently combined with office workers to produce a final good. By defining an aggregate neoclassical production function that nests this real estate sector, they mathematically prove that if the elasticity of substitution between land and non-land factors *within the real estate sector* exceeds one ($\sigma_E > 1$), the aggregate elasticity of substitution also asymptotically exceeds one ($\sigma_F > 1$) at high input levels. Consequently, the general theorem remains completely valid: land overvaluation inevitably emerges in urban formations as long as there is continuous technological progress in real estate construction that outpaces fixed land productivity.

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: The urban-land application extends the general theorem by proving that land overvaluation inevitably occurs in modern cities if the elasticity of substitution in the intermediate real estate construction sector exceeds one and construction technology improves.
- **Mechanism**:
  - The urban economy is modeled with a nested structure: a real estate sector combines construction labor and land, and a final goods sector combines office labor and real estate.
  - This nested, multi-sector structure is mathematically reduced to a composite aggregate production function.
  - If the elasticity of substitution between land and labor in producing real estate is strictly greater than one ($\sigma_E > 1$), the composite aggregate elasticity of substitution also asymptotically exceeds one ($\sigma_F > 1$).
  - Therefore, as technological progress in construction outpaces the fixed productivity of urban land, aggregate unbalanced growth is triggered, ensuring that urban land prices decouple from their fundamental rents and generate overvaluation.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Proposition 3, establishing the relationship $\sigma_F - 1 = \frac{1}{1 + \alpha \frac{XE_X}{HE_H} \sigma_E}(\sigma_E - 1)$, which formally proves that $\sigma_E > 1$ implies $\sigma_F > 1$.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: land overvaluation
- **Critical caveat**: Applying this highly formalized urban land framework to Latin America requires external verification, as the model assumes formal construction technologies and abstracts from the region's massive informal, self-built urban settlements and distinct spatial segregation patterns.
