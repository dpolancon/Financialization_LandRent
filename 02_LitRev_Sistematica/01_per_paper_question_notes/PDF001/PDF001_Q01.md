## NotebookLM question

What problem does the paper call the disconnection between land’s declining productive role and land’s continuing role as a store of value?

## NotebookLM answer

> The paper refers to this issue as the **"apparent disconnection between land output and land value"** [1].
>  
> The authors note a historical trend where land's importance as a factor of production has steadily declined—seen in the shrinking GDP and employment shares of land-intensive industries like agriculture [1, 2]. At the same time, land has continued to serve as a scarce and prominent "store of value," making up more than 50% of household wealth in many major countries [1, 3].
>  
> To explain this problem, the paper theoretically studies the long-run behavior of land prices and identifies that this disconnection results in **land overvaluation** [1]. Through their **Land Overvaluation Theorem**, the authors demonstrate that land inevitably becomes overvalued relative to its fundamental value (the present value of its rents) when two conditions are met:
> 		1. **Unbalanced growth:** Technological progress is faster in non-land sectors (like the modern knowledge and service economies) than in land-intensive sectors [1, 4].
> 		2. **Elasticity of substitution:** The elasticity of substitution between land and non-land factors exceeds 1, meaning that as economies develop, other factors (like building taller buildings on fixed land) can substitute for land [1].
>  
> Ultimately, because people continue to use land as a primary means of saving while wages grow faster than land rents, land prices are pulled upward by growing incomes faster than the actual productive output of the land can justify [5]..
