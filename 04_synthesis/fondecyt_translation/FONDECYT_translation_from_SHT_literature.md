# Fondecyt translation from the SHT land-finance literature

## 1. Purpose of this bridge note

This note separates project translation from literature synthesis. The SHT/Hirano-Stiglitz-Toda corpus gives the Fondecyt project a rigorous set of mechanisms connecting land, credit, asset prices, capital formation, and growth. It does not by itself supply a complete theory of Latin American land-rent financialization, localization rents, urban informality, peripheral finance, or local institutional histories.

The purpose is to identify what can be used directly, what must be translated, and what should remain downstream policy material rather than a premise of the project's formal model.

## 2. What the project can directly use

The project can directly use the conceptual separation between land, real estate, land rent, housing rent, land price, measured wealth, and productive capital. This is the foundation for avoiding empirical conflation: a rise in land or real-estate value is not automatically productive capital accumulation.

The project can also use the mechanism that land acts simultaneously as a non-produced asset, store of value, collateral object, and speculative object. That framing helps connect land-price inflation to credit expansion, collateral values, leverage, overvaluation, and the diversion of savings from productive capital.

The most useful direct mechanisms are:

- credit expansion or collateral relaxation directed toward land/real estate can raise land prices and measured wealth;
- higher land prices can absorb savings that otherwise finance productive capital;
- land prices can diverge from land rents under unbalanced growth;
- housing rent data can hide pure land-rent dynamics;
- sectoral credit composition matters more than aggregate credit volume for long-run growth.

## 3. What requires translation before use

The SHT mechanisms require translation before they are used in Latin American or Fondecyt-specific claims. The project must specify how land tenure, informality, urban land-use rules, zoning, cadastral systems, banking structure, mortgage depth, foreign capital flows, commodity dependence, and local state capacity alter the model channels.

The project's language of land usage, localization rents, and financialization is not identical to the SHT model vocabulary. In SHT, land is often a fixed/non-produced asset in OLG or macro-finance models. In the Fondecyt agenda, land rent may also be shaped by urban location, infrastructure, planning decisions, elite ownership, and peripheral financial conditions. Those additions should be presented as theoretical translation, not as claims already established by the SHT corpus.

## 4. What should not be imported uncritically

The project should not import the closed-economy OLG framework as if it were a direct description of Latin American urban land markets. It should not assume formal credit access, homogeneous land, perfect cadastral observability, frictionless land markets, or clean substitution between land and structures without checking institutional context.

The project should also avoid importing SHT policy conclusions as immediate recommendations. Land-rent taxation, capital-gains taxation, collateral regulation, and public retention of land returns are analytically useful, but their implementation depends on administrative capacity, political economy, ownership concentration, and legal structure.

Finally, the project should not conflate rational-expectations bubbles with behavioral, institutional, or political-economy accounts of speculation. SHT provides one formal route to land overvaluation and instability; it should be distinguished from other literatures rather than used as a universal explanation.

## 5. Mechanisms relevant for the project's formal modeling

The strongest formal-modeling sequence for the project is:

credit expansion or relaxed real-estate collateral -> higher land/real-estate financing -> higher land prices and collateral values -> speculative demand and leverage -> diversion of savings or credit away from productive capital -> lower capital formation, productivity, or growth.

A second useful sequence is:

unbalanced productivity growth -> land's productive rent grows more slowly than aggregate income or savings -> land remains a store of value -> land prices outpace land rents -> overvaluation or bubble-like dynamics.

A third sequence is:

land as collateral -> higher land price -> higher pledgeable value -> further credit creation -> stronger asset-price feedback.

These mechanisms can inform the Fondecyt model if the project specifies local equivalents for land-price measures, pure land rents, real-estate credit, collateral rules, productive investment, and growth outcomes.

## 6. Policy material: useful but analytically downstream

The Georgist land-rent capture material is important, but it should enter the Fondecyt work downstream of the causal architecture. First, the project should establish how land rent becomes financialized and how that affects investment, credit, and growth. Then it can evaluate policies such as land-rent taxation, capital-gains taxation, collateral restrictions, public investment, or public retention of land returns.

The policy cluster `03_notes/clusters/C6_georgist_land_rent_capture_regulation.md` should be cited for the literature's policy mechanisms. This bridge note should be used to decide which of those mechanisms fit the Fondecyt research design.

## 7. Relation to the five review dimensions

For definitions, SHT contributes clear separations among land, real estate, land rent, housing rent, land price, wealth, and productive capital.

For mechanisms, SHT contributes credit-collateral channels, land-price feedback loops, crowding out of productive capital, unbalanced-growth overvaluation, and wobbly instability.

For causality, SHT contributes conditional model claims: sectoral credit composition, collateral parameters, elasticity of substitution, productivity-growth gaps, and savings allocation determine whether land speculation harms capital formation.

For variables and parameters, SHT contributes `P_t`, land rents or dividends, `K_t`, `k_{t+1}`, `r`, `R`, `G`, `theta`, `theta^x`, leverage, `sigma`, productivity growth, wealth `W`, and policy tax parameters.

For critical evaluation, SHT contributes a strong formal core but leaves open institutional translation, empirical measurement, Latin American specificity, and comparison with political economy, urban, structuralist, Keynesian, behavioral, and financialization literatures.

## 8. Implications for the memo/draft

The memo should use SHT as a disciplined mechanism base. It should begin with conceptual separations, move to land-credit and land-price mechanisms, then address overvaluation and growth effects, and only after that discuss policy implications. The Fondecyt translation should be explicit: "SHT shows a formal mechanism; the project asks how that mechanism is modified by Latin American land, credit, and urban institutions."

The memo should avoid presenting Fondecyt translation as if it were already contained in the original models. Where the old notes mention Latin America as requiring verification, the draft should preserve that caution.

## 9. Open tasks before drafting

The project still needs to map SHT variables onto available empirical proxies: pure land rent, housing rent, land price, real-estate value, credit by sector, collateral regulation, productive investment, productivity, and growth.

It also needs to decide which external literatures will supplement SHT: Latin American urban political economy, land-rent theory, financialization, structuralism, dependency/peripheral finance, housing informality, planning and zoning, and state-capacity literature.

Before final drafting, page references for formulas and policy propositions should be checked against the underlying paper notes. Where source notes lack precise page references, the claim should retain a missing-page flag or be excluded from quotation-level prose.
