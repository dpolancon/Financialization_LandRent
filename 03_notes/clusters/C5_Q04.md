# C5_Q04 — Land, R versus G, and infinite debt rollover

> [!question]
> What exactly is wrong with applying the Diamond landless rollover intuition to an economy with land?

## NotebookLM answer

[NOTEBOOKLM_CLUSTER]

**1. The Cross-Paper Answer**
Applying the Diamond (1965) landless intuition to an economy with land is fundamentally flawed because **the mere existence of land alters the economic conditions under which infinite debt rollover (a Ponzi scheme) is possible** [1-4]. The conventional Diamond model asserts that infinite debt rollover is possible if and only if the economy suffers from dynamic inefficiency ($R < G$, leading to a Pareto inefficient equilibrium) [2, 4-7]. However, when a non-reproducible, productive asset like land is introduced in a low-interest-rate environment, bubbleless equilibria cease to exist [8, 9]. Instead, **a land price bubble inevitably emerges, which simultaneously achieves Pareto efficiency** [2, 4, 10, 11]. Thus, in a land economy, dynamic inefficiency is irrelevant; sustainable infinite debt rollover is possible *despite* the economy being Pareto efficient, because the debt essentially absorbs part of the inevitable land bubble [2, 4, 12, 13].

**2. Key Mechanisms and Variables**
*   **Variables:** The critical parameters are the interest rate ($R$), the economic growth rate ($G$), the land dividend/rent growth rate ($G_r$), the land price ($P_t$), its fundamental value ($V_t$), and the elasticity of substitution between labor and land ($\sigma$) [14-19].
*   **The Bubble Necessity Mechanism:** If the natural interest rate is lower than the dividend growth rate ($R < G_r < G$), the fundamental value of land becomes infinite, which is impossible in equilibrium [8, 9, 14, 17]. Therefore, the land price must contain a bubble ($P_t > V_t$). A fiscal authority can sustain infinite deficits by issuing debt that proportionally replaces a fraction of this necessary bubble, rolling it over indefinitely at the market interest rate [15, 18, 20-23].
*   **Unbalanced Growth Requirement:** For infinite debt rollover to occur in a land economy, the economy must exhibit **unbalanced growth** [24-27]. This requires labor productivity to grow faster than land productivity and the elasticity of substitution to be greater than one ($\sigma > 1$) [28-31]. Under standard balanced growth (where wages and rents grow at the same rate), land prices grow proportionately with output, structurally forcing $R > G$ and making Ponzi schemes strictly impossible [32-36].

**3. Tensions, Limits, and Open Questions**
*   **Tensions:** There is a direct theoretical tension with McCallum (1987), who claimed that land universally guarantees $R > G$ and rules out Pareto inefficiency entirely [32, 35, 37, 38]. The sources resolve this by proving McCallum's result is a "knife-edge" case that only holds if the production function is strictly Cobb-Douglas ($\sigma = 1$) or if labor and land productivities grow at exactly the same rate [39-42]. 
*   **Limits and Open Questions:** The models presented are deterministic and theoretical [43, 44]. While they prove that public debt can displace part of a land bubble, the exact limits of how much debt can be absorbed before the bubble bursts remain abstract. *(Note: Latin American applications—such as how these rollover dynamics operate within highly unequal, highly financialized, or institutionally distinct Latin American land markets—are not covered in the theoretical models provided and require independent verification).*

**4. Relevance to the final 5-15 page memo**
This synthesis provides a critical theoretical pillar for the Fondecyt memo on land-rent financialization. It explicitly demonstrates that **macroeconomic models assessing debt sustainability and financialization cannot ignore land** [25, 27]. It justifies framing modern land-rent financialization through the lens of *unbalanced growth* ($\sigma > 1$), proving that the underlying mechanisms driving debt rollover and asset overvaluation in land-inclusive economies are structurally opposed to the classical capital-only frameworks [34, 36, 45, 46].
