## 3. Core thesis

- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF004_Q01` (3, 4, 9, 17, 34, 49): The paper organizes a four-part theoretical research program demonstrating that market economies are intrinsically prone to expectation-driven instability, and that the presence of land and credit frictions causes speculative real estate booms to structurally crowd out productive capital, lowering long-run endogenous growth.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF004_Q02` (1, 8, 25, 26): Multiple momentary equilibria allow an economy to endogenously switch between "bullish" and "bearish" states driven by self-fulfilling expectations, generating an infinite number of bounded rational-expectations trajectories (wobbly dynamics) rather than smooth convergence to a unique steady state.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF004_Q03` (3, 17): Introducing land as a competing store of value expands the mathematical bounds for multiple momentary equilibria, generating a richer set of endogenous fluctuations and increasing overall macroeconomic variability.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF004_Q04` (1, 40, 43, 44, 48): Credit frictions interact with land speculation to suppress economic growth, because expansionary credit policies that favor real estate divert finite entrepreneurial funds away from the productive capital investments necessary for endogenous growth.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF004_Q05` (3, 5, 15, 36, 49): The paper functions strictly as an overview and synthesis of the authors' broader research program, parsimoniously adapting models from their 2022 and 2024 papers rather than introducing novel formal results.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF004_Q06` (3, 4): The paper acts as a direct structural guide for mapping the research program, as it deliberately synthesizes the authors' prior technical papers into a simplified, four-part escalating narrative about macroeconomic instability and land speculation.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF004_Q07` (3, 4, 5, 15, 36): The paper's first two parts (basic OLG instability and land introduction) serve as theoretical background, while the final two parts (endogenous growth and credit frictions) provide the core results regarding how financial easing suppresses long-term growth.

- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF004_Q03`: The framework’s generation of these expanded fluctuations relies strictly on a closed OLG economy driven entirely by domestic life-cycle savings.
