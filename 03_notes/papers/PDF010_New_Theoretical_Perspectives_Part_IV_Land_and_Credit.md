## 3. Core thesis

- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF010_Q01` (16, 17, 18): Part IV explains the "wealth residual" by showing that credit expansion, explicitly secured by land collateral, artificially inflates land prices and measured wealth while contributing nothing to—and potentially crowding out—real productive capital accumulation.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF010_Q02` (1, 3, 4, 13, 17): Increases in the wealth-to-income ratio and wealth inequality are driven by the rising market valuation of land—fueled by capitalized rents, competition for positional goods, and speculative bubbles—which creates massive capital gains for the wealthy while crowding out real productive investment.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF010_Q03` (4, 16, 18, 20, 22): Easing monetary policy and financial regulations (like lower collateral and capital adequacy requirements) fuels a credit expansion that artificially inflates land prices, generating massive capital gains for wealthy asset owners and worsening inequality without improving real economic efficiency.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF010_Q04` (8, 12, 13, 14, 19): Land accumulation crowds out real capital accumulation because rising land prices act as a substitute for real investment, absorbing finite macroeconomic savings into non-productive capital gains.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF010_Q05` (4, 5, 14): Land taxation reduces the divergence between measured wealth and productive capital by decreasing the capitalized value of land rents and redirecting speculative savings back into real capital accumulation.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF010_Q06` (8, 13, 18): While explicitly connecting this text to later Hirano-Stiglitz models requires external verification, the paper lays the foundational theoretical mechanism for credit-land-growth frameworks by showing that credit expansion tied to land collateral fuels asset bubbles that crowd out real capital accumulation.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF010_Q07` (15, 16, 17, 20, 25): Credit-led financialization exacerbates inequality and crowds out productive investment because banks create credit based on fixed collateral (land), which artificially inflates asset prices and enriches wealthy owners without contributing to the real economy.

## 8. Formal results / model results

- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF010_Q01` (16, 17, 18): The credit-collateral equation (4.10) $c = \alpha pT$, and the wealth-credit derivative (4.7) $dW/dc > 0$, demonstrating that wealth increases with credit expansion while the equilibrium capital stock remains completely unaffected.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF010_Q02` (1, 3, 4, 13, 17): The land-rent wealth equation $W = K + R/r$ and the positional-good capital equation $K = W - p(W)$, both demonstrating that aggregate wealth ($W$) can increase significantly due to land price appreciation while productive capital ($K$) stagnates or declines.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF010_Q03` (4, 16, 18, 20, 22): The collateral-credit equation $c = \alpha pT$ (4.10) and the leveraged return on land equation $\rho_T = (1+\alpha)(\frac{d}{dt}(\log p) + \frac{F_T}{p})$ (4.16), demonstrating that changing the rules of the game for collateral ($\alpha$) directly drives up land prices.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF010_Q04` (8, 12, 13, 14, 19): The savings allocation equation (3.2), $\frac{dK}{dt} + T\frac{dp}{dt} = s(F_K K - \mu K + T\frac{dp}{dt})$, which demonstrates that net savings must cover both capital accumulation and the increasing value of land.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF010_Q05` (4, 5, 14): Equation (1.2') $W = K + \frac{(1-\tau^L)R}{r} = K + (1-\tau^L)R/F_K$, demonstrating that an increased tax rate on land returns ($\tau^L$) directly aligns wealth ($W$) and capital ($K$).
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF010_Q06` (8, 13, 18): The collateral-credit equation $c = \alpha pT$ (4.10) and the resulting dynamic where rapid credit expansion causes capital per capita ($k$) to decline: $d(\log k)/dt = -n$ (4.14).
