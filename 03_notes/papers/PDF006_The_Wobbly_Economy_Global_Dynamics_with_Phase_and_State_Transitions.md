## 3. Core thesis

- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF006_Q01` (1, 2, 10): Wobbly macro-dynamics describes a rational-expectations economy that infinitely fluctuates without converging or displaying regular periodicity, fundamentally challenging standard RBC and Solow models that predict smooth convergence to a unique steady state.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF006_Q02` (2, 8, 9, 15, 16): Multiplicity of momentary equilibria arises when an overlapping generations economy features strong gross complementarity in both consumption (low intertemporal elasticity of substitution) and production (low elasticity of substitution between capital and labor), causing the aggregate savings curve to become non-monotonic with respect to interest rates.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF006_Q03` (1, 2, 3, 27): Phase transitions are endogenous dynamic movements between unique and multiple equilibrium regimes along a given trajectory, whereas state transitions are exogenous structural shifts caused by parameter changes that redefine the macroeconomic system's overall stability.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF006_Q04` (24, 25, 26, 49): Positive productivity shocks can paradoxically destabilize an economy by shifting it into a regime of multiple equilibria, where pessimistic expectations can trigger a collapse into a permanent stagnation trap, generating strong hysteresis even from temporary technological improvements.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF006_Q05` (2, 3, 4, 15, 21, 36): The baseline paper proves that even without introducing land or credit, a standard overlapping generations economy naturally generates endless, expectation-driven macroeconomic instability ("wobbly dynamics") rather than smooth convergence to a steady state.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF006_Q06` (4, 32): The core concepts required for the later land-speculation models are phase transitions and state transitions driven by endogenous state variables, whereas dynamic inefficiency is explicitly identified as an unnecessary condition for generating wobbly dynamics.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF006_Q07` (2, 8, 10): The model is easily explained by demonstrating how finite-lived agents who reduce their savings when interest rates rise create a system with multiple momentary equilibria, meaning endless economic fluctuations ("wobbles") are driven purely by self-fulfilling optimistic or pessimistic expectations.

## 11. Limits and caveats

- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF006_Q01`: The authors explicitly state this is an exercise in pure theory modeling a closed economy without money, credit, or real estate; applying this purely domestic, expectation-driven volatility framework to Latin American business cycles requires external verification regarding the region's specific vulnerabilities to exogenous global financial shocks and foreign capital flows.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF006_Q02`: The model's generation of multiple equilibria strictly assumes a closed economy driven by domestic life-cycle savings, completely abstracting from money, land, and credit; applying these specific elasticity-driven wobbly dynamics to Latin America requires external verification regarding the region's exposure to international capital flows and foreign exchange constraints.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF006_Q03`: The model’s distinction between these transitions relies on a closed-economy framework where transitions are driven purely by domestic capital accumulation or domestic technological parameters; applying these exact dynamics to Latin America requires external verification regarding the region's exposure to exogenous global financial shocks, foreign debt crises, and terms-of-trade shifts.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF006_Q04`: The model explains instability and hysteresis strictly through closed-economy domestic life-cycle savings dynamics; applying these expectation-driven technological shock mechanisms to Latin America requires external verification regarding the region's exposure to exogenous global financial shocks, foreign debt cycles, and commodity terms-of-trade shifts.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF006_Q05`: The baseline model relies strictly on a closed economy driven by domestic life-cycle savings to generate its wobbly dynamics; applying this purely domestic, expectation-driven instability to Latin America requires external verification regarding the region's exposure to exogenous global financial shocks and foreign capital flows.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF006_Q06`: Because this baseline model acts as an abstract theoretical prototype based on a closed economy, applying its distinction between essential phase transitions and optional dynamic inefficiency to Latin America requires external verification regarding the region's specific history of dollarized real estate speculation and reliance on foreign capital.
