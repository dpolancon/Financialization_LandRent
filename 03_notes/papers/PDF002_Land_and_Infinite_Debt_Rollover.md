## 3. Core thesis

- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF002_Q01` (1, 2, 16, 17): The paper refutes the conventional McCallum/Homburg claim that land unconditionally guarantees Pareto efficiency ($R>G$), proving that their result depends on knife-edge balanced-growth restrictions and that infinite debt rollover remains possible under realistic unbalanced growth conditions.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF002_Q02` (8, 13): Land bubbles inevitably emerge when high savings motives and unbalanced growth drive the natural interest rate below the growth rate of land dividends ($R < G_r < G$), preventing fundamental asset values from reaching infinity.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF002_Q03` (2, 9, 10): Infinite debt rollover is possible in a Pareto efficient land economy because the fiscal authority can sustainably issue public debt to absorb a proportional share of an inevitably emerging, efficient land price bubble.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF002_Q04` (3, 14, 20): Unbalanced growth is theoretically necessary for infinite debt rollover in an economy with land because balanced growth structurally forces the interest rate above the economic growth rate ($R > G$), rendering any Ponzi scheme unsustainable.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF002_Q05` (8, 9, 15, 16, 17): The CES production example proves that McCallum's "land efficiency" result hinges on a knife-edge mathematical restriction ($\sigma = 1$), and demonstrates that relaxing it to allow unbalanced growth ($\sigma > 1$) permits Pareto inefficient equilibria and sustainable infinite debt rollover.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF002_Q06` (1-3, 7-9, 13-14, 20): The presence of land fundamentally alters the conditions for infinite debt rollover; rather than relying on a Pareto inefficient $R<G$ state, debt rollover in a land economy is sustained by the inevitable emergence of land bubbles under Pareto efficient, unbalanced growth.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF002_Q07` (3, 9, 18, 20): The existence of land fundamentally alters the conditions for infinite public debt rollover, making it possible either through the inevitable emergence of Pareto-efficient land bubbles or through Pareto-inefficient equilibria driven by unbalanced growth [4-6].

## 11. Limits and caveats

- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF002_Q01`: Applying this theoretical critique of the McCallum/Homburg result to Latin American sovereign debt requires external verification, as the paper's framework is a deterministic, closed-economy overlapping-generations model that completely abstracts from the region's exposure to open-economy capital flight, dollarization, and high sovereign risk premiums.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF002_Q02`: Applying these precise demographic and unbalanced-growth conditions to Latin America requires external verification, as the framework relies on a deterministic, closed-economy OLG model and completely abstracts from the region's peripheral realities, such as dollarized urban real estate, sovereign risk premiums, and vulnerability to sudden stops in foreign capital.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF002_Q03`: Applying this closed-economy, deterministic mechanism for debt rollover to Latin America requires external verification, as the model entirely abstracts from the region's exposure to aggregate uncertainty, sovereign risk premiums, capital flight, and foreign-currency-denominated public debt.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF002_Q04`: Applying the necessity of unbalanced growth for debt rollover to Latin America requires external verification, as the region's sovereign debt sustainability is frequently dictated by external shocks, foreign-currency denomination, and sudden stops in capital flows, rather than the deterministic, closed-economy productivity differentials modeled here.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF002_Q05`: Applying this theoretical revision via CES production mechanics to Latin America requires external verification, as the stylized formalization abstracts entirely from the region's structural heterogeneity, informal labor markets, commodity-export dependence, and external debt vulnerabilities.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF002_Q06`: For Latin American applicability, this closed-economy framework structurally abstracts from the reality that peripheral sovereign debt rollover is constrained primarily by exogenous global financial shocks, foreign exchange bottlenecks, and core-economy monetary policy, rather than domestic life-cycle savings and endogenous land bubbles.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF002_Q07`: Latin American applicability caveat: The model relies on the absence of aggregate uncertainty and assumes no property taxes or real estate transaction costs; specific regional applications require external verification because such frictions invalidate the theoretical assumption that land is a frictionless, non-depreciating asset.
