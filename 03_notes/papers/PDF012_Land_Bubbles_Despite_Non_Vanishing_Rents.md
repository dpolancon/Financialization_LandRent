## 3. Core thesis

- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF012_Q01` (1, 2, 3): The paper refutes the empirical objection that stationary housing rent-yields rule out rational bubbles, demonstrating that land bubbles inevitably emerge even when the aggregate housing rent-to-price ratio is constant.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF012_Q02` (1, 2, 3): Separating housing rent from pure land rent is essential because the empirical stationarity of overall housing rent yields masks the vanishing pure land dividend, causing researchers to incorrectly dismiss the existence of rational land bubbles.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF012_Q03` (1, 2, 3): A land bubble can exist despite a constant overall housing rent yield because the total housing rent is dominated by the return on reproducible structures; once separated, the underlying pure land dividend yield mathematically vanishes, allowing the bubble to form.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF012_Q04` (1, 2, 3): The minimal model is a two-period OLG framework that explicitly separates housing into reproducible structures and non-reproducible land, fundamentally requiring an elasticity of substitution greater than one between these two inputs for land bubbles to inevitably emerge.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF012_Q05` (1, 2, 3): The paper dictates that empirical projects must not use stationary aggregate housing rent-to-price ratios to rule out bubbles, but must instead explicitly isolate pure land rents from the rents accruing to reproducible housing structures.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF012_Q06` (1, 2, 3): Latin American real-estate datasets that combine structure and land rents will likely exhibit stationary gross rent yields, which mathematically masks the vanishing pure land dividend and can lead researchers to incorrectly conclude that no land bubble exists.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF012_Q07` (1, 2, 3): The final memo should feature a strict measurement warning that using aggregate housing rent-to-price ratios as an empirical indicator will falsely rule out land bubbles; researchers must explicitly isolate pure land rents from structural housing returns.
