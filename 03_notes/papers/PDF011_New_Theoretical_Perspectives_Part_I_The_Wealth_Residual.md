## 3. Core thesis

- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF011_Q01` (10, 11, 28): The wealth residual represents the massive gap between the observed surge in aggregate wealth and the actual, much lower rate of real capital accumulation dictated by national savings data, indicating that wealth increases are driven by capitalized rents rather than productive investment.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF011_Q02` (7, 16, 17, 18, 21): Wealth and capital are fundamentally different aggregates because measured wealth includes not just productive capital but also the capitalized value of rents (like land and monopoly exploitation), explaining how aggregate wealth can soar while true productive capacity shrinks.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF011_Q03` (1, 21, 22, 23, 25): The massive divergence between measured wealth and productive capital (the wealth residual) is driven by the capitalization of non-productive land rents, exploitation/market-power rents, and intellectual-property rents.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF011_Q04` (4, 14, 16): The new stylized facts empirically contradict standard capital-labor models, which falsely predict that the observed massive increases in wealth (if interpreted as true productive capital) would force higher average wages, a lower return to capital, and a higher labor share.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF011_Q05` (6, 9): The foundational elements for later land-credit models are the conceptualization of land as a non-produced, collateralizable asset and the insight that financial and monetary policies directly inflate the value of this collateral, thereby driving wealth inequality.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF011_Q06` (8, 10, 16, 18): To avoid overloading the memo with distribution theory, the review should exclusively extract the paper's "wealth residual" framework, which proves that soaring wealth-income ratios are driven by the capitalized value of land and exploitation rents rather than genuine productive capital.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF011_Q07` (24, 25, 29, 30): The surge in measured wealth and inequality is structurally driven by politically mediated "exploitation rents" and financialization, wherein the financial sector acts as an extractive apparatus rather than a productive intermediary.

## 11. Limits and caveats

- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF011_Q01`: Evaluating the exact magnitude and drivers of the wealth residual in Latin America requires external verification, as the paper’s empirical parameters rely strictly on US and advanced-economy macroeconomic data, abstracting from the region's specific vulnerabilities to foreign capital inflows, commodity price shocks, and structural external debt.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF011_Q02`: Applying this precise measurement distinction between wealth and capital to Latin American economies requires external verification, as the paper relies on advanced-economy data (US, France, UK) and abstracts from the region's specific dependence on commodity export rents, volatile foreign capital flows, and sovereign debt.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF011_Q03`: Evaluating the relative importance of these three specific rent channels in Latin America requires external verification, as the paper’s framework is based on advanced economies and does not explicitly address the region's heavy reliance on commodity-export rents, peripheral financial constraints, or imported intellectual property.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF011_Q04`: Applying this critique of standard capital-labor models to Latin America requires external verification, as the paper relies entirely on advanced-economy stylized facts (e.g., US wage stagnation and wealth-income ratios) and does not model the region's specific structural dependencies, such as primary commodity exports, informality, and peripheral financial constraints.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF011_Q05`: Applying these foundational land-credit concepts to Latin America requires external verification, as the paper relies on advanced-economy stylized facts and abstracts from the region's specific peripheral financial constraints, such as dollarized real estate markets and reliance on foreign capital inflows.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF011_Q06`: Applying this precise wealth-capital critique to Latin America requires external verification, as the paper's empirical framing relies entirely on advanced-economy data (US, France, UK) and abstracts from the region's peripheral financial constraints, informal labor markets, and commodity-driven land regimes.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF011_Q07`: Connecting this political-economy framework to Latin America requires external verification, as the paper's critique of financialization relies heavily on advanced-economy institutional contexts (e.g., US corporate governance, global "too-big-to-fail" banking) and abstracts from peripheral dynamics like dependency on foreign capital flows or external debt constraints.
