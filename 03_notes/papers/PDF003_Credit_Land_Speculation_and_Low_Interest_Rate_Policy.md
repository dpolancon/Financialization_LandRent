## 3. Core thesis

- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF003_Q01` (1): The macroeconomic impact of a credit boom depends strictly on its sectoral composition, as credit expansions directed toward real estate systematically crowd out productive commercial investment and retard long-term economic growth.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF003_Q02` (1, 2, 16, 29): The macroeconomic impact of a credit expansion depends on its sectoral target: expanding real estate credit retards long-term economic growth, whereas expanding productive capital credit enhances it.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF003_Q03` (2, 10, 11): When land acts as an alternative store of value, speculative real estate booms divert finite domestic savings away from productive investments, generating a general-equilibrium crowding-out effect that lowers capital formation and long-term economic growth.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF003_Q04` (2, 16, 17, 19, 29): In an economy with land, low-interest-rate policies do not automatically boost productive investment; instead, they induce a portfolio shift toward real estate speculation that crowds out capital formation and lowers long-run growth.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF003_Q05` (2, 21-23): Speculative asset booms generate a severe intergenerational inequality, enriching the initial generation of asset holders and borrowers while permanently impoverishing future generations through lower wages and stunted economic growth.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF003_Q06` (3, 21-22 (PDF003); 327, 334-335 (PDF005)): PDF003 frames the crowding-out problem around monetary easing and intergenerational welfare (refuting the Tobin effect), distinguishing itself from PDF013's broader focus on general credit expansions and PDF005's institutional focus on Georgist fiscal policies and land reform.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF003_Q07` (2, 16, 27, 28): The macroeconomic impact of private credit expansion depends on its sectoral allocation; credit expansions that fuel land-price inflation crowd out productive capital investment, generating temporary wealth illusions while retarding long-term growth.

## 11. Limits and caveats

- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF003_Q01`: The source does not specifically address Latin American economies; applying these empirical patterns to Latin American debates requires external verification regarding the region's specific sectoral credit composition, reliance on primary exports, and historical land concentration.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF003_Q02`: The paper assumes a closed economy where domestic life-cycle savings finance all investment; applying these dynamics to Latin American real estate and credit cycles requires external verification regarding the region's reliance on foreign capital inflows, sovereign debt constraints, and historic land concentration (*latifundia*).
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF003_Q03`: Applying this mechanism to Latin American economies requires external verification, as the model structurally assumes a closed economy reliant strictly on domestic life-cycle savings rather than foreign capital inflows, and it abstracts from the region's specific history of concentrated land ownership and extractivism.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF003_Q04`: The paper strictly models a closed domestic economy where portfolio allocation is determined by the life-cycle savings of domestic entrepreneurs; applying these monetary transmission mechanisms to Latin America requires external verification regarding the region's reliance on foreign capital flows, exogenous global interest rates, and historical land concentration.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF003_Q05`: The model assumes a closed overlapping-generations economy where intergenerational welfare is strictly mediated by domestic wages and life-cycle savings. Applying these exact distributional outcomes to Latin America requires external verification regarding the region's reliance on foreign capital, external sovereign debt burdens passed to future generations, and historic land concentration.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF003_Q06`: Applying these differing policy prescriptions to Latin America requires external verification to determine whether a specific country's primary macroeconomic constraint is historical land concentration requiring Georgist fiscal reform (PDF005) or speculative credit and monetary cycles requiring macroprudential regulation (PDF003/PDF013).
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF003_Q07`: The source explicitly models a closed economy where all asset purchases are financed strictly by domestic life-cycle savings.
