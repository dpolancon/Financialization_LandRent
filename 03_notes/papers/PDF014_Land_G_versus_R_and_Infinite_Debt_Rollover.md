## 3. Core thesis

- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF014_Q01` (4): The CIGS version adds an explicit critique of the $R < G$ heuristic, arguing that even if market frictions allow $R < G$ in a land model, the simple $G$ versus $R$ condition loses its theoretical coherence and is no longer a sufficient metric for determining the sustainability of infinite debt rollover.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF014_Q02` (2, 8, 9): The paper proves that in an economy with land experiencing unbalanced growth, infinite debt rollover is feasible even when the equilibrium is Pareto efficient, fundamentally breaking the conventional reliance on the $R < G$ dynamic inefficiency condition.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF014_Q03` (2, 4, 14): Balanced growth in a land economy guarantees that the interest rate strictly exceeds the economic growth rate ($R > G$), rendering infinite debt rollover impossible because public debt would unsustainably outpace economic growth.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF014_Q04` (7, 9, 12, 13): Land bubbles and infinite debt rollover inevitably emerge under unbalanced growth when the elasticity of substitution between labor and land exceeds one ($\sigma > 1$), labor productivity outpaces land productivity, and high savings motives drive the natural interest rate below the dividend growth rate.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF014_Q05` (2, 3, 16, 17, 19): The paper refutes McCallum’s generalized claim that land guarantees Pareto efficiency by proving his result depends on knife-edge balanced-growth restrictions, showing instead that Pareto inefficient equilibria can emerge under realistic unbalanced growth conditions.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF014_Q06` (2, 4, 9, 19): The literature review must argue that $R < G$ is not a theoretically coherent or sufficient condition for infinite debt rollover, because introducing land demonstrates that sustainable debt rollover can occur in perfectly Pareto-efficient economies where dynamic inefficiency is absent.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF014_Q07` (4, 8, 9): Applying the model to Latin America requires external verification, raising critical open questions about how a deterministic, closed-economy mechanism for infinite debt rollover can function amid the region's sovereign risk and aggregate uncertainty.

## 11. Limits and caveats

- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF014_Q01`: Because this critique of the $G$ versus $R$ framework is highly theoretical and generalized, applying it to Latin America requires external verification to determine how the region's specific peripheral frictions—such as sovereign risk premiums, dollarized real estate, and informal property markets—interact with debt rollover sustainability.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF014_Q02`: Applying this theoretical reformulation of debt rollover to Latin America requires external verification, as the model relies on a closed overlapping-generations framework and abstracts from the region's actual limits on debt sustainability, such as sovereign risk premiums, foreign-currency denominated debt, and vulnerability to capital flight.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF014_Q03`: Applying this balanced-growth impossibility theorem to Latin America requires external verification, as the region's economies are frequently characterized by structural volatility, external shocks, and unbalanced growth dynamics that deviate significantly from the steady-state assumptions of this model.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF014_Q04`: Applying these precise unbalanced-growth prerequisites to Latin America requires external verification, as the theoretical model relies on formalized CES production mechanics and abstracts from the region's structural heterogeneity, informal labor dynamics, and commodity-export-driven land valuations.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF014_Q05`: Applying this theoretical finding regarding Pareto inefficiency to Latin America requires external verification, as the paper relies on a stylized, closed-economy overlapping-generations model and completely abstracts from the region's peripheral macroeconomic realities, such as sovereign risk premiums, foreign capital dependence, and structural external debt limits.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF014_Q06`: Asserting the irrelevance of the $R < G$ heuristic for Latin American sovereign debt requires external verification, as the region's debt sustainability is frequently constrained by exogenous peripheral vulnerabilities—such as foreign currency denomination, "sudden stops" in capital flows, and sovereign risk premiums—rather than the closed-economy overlapping-generations mechanics modeled here.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF014_Q07`: Because the paper entirely abstracts from open-economy dynamics, sovereign default risk, and external debt, any application of its infinite debt rollover mechanisms to Latin American fiscal regimes requires external verification.
