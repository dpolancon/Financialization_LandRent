## 3. Core thesis

- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF007_Q01` (7, 24, 46, 47): Introducing land as an alternative asset fundamentally alters macroeconomic dynamics by crowding out productive capital, which lowers wages and output while expanding the conditions under which the economy experiences endless, expectation-driven boom-bust cycles.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF007_Q02` (2, 7, 9): Rising land prices trigger a self-reinforcing feedback loop that crowds out real capital accumulation, driving up interest rates and necessitating even faster, ultimately unsustainable exponential increases in land prices.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF007_Q03` (1, 2, 3, 13, 19): Seemingly explosive land booms are perfectly consistent with rational expectations because the multiplicity of momentary equilibria guarantees that prices will endogenously crash before reaching an impossible, infinitely explosive threshold.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF007_Q04` (19, 20): Critical land-price thresholds ($\hat{P}$ and $\underline{P}$) force endogenous phase transitions between bullish and bearish states, ensuring that speculative trajectories remain mathematically bounded and consistent with rational expectations rather than exploding to infinity or imploding to zero.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF007_Q05` (2, 7, 10): Land acts as a non-produced asset that absorbs finite domestic savings, directly displacing productive capital investment and structurally expanding the mathematical conditions under which the economy suffers from expectation-driven instability and boom-bust cycles.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF007_Q06` (41, 42, 43, 44): The paper demonstrates that while standard land and capital gains taxes can paradoxically increase macroeconomic volatility, targeted capital taxes/subsidies and credible full-commitment tax regimes can successfully eliminate wobbly dynamics, reduce capital crowding-out, and maximize social welfare.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF007_Q07` (7, 9): The model's savings and capital arbitrage equations jointly demonstrate that land-price inflation mechanically crowds out productive capital formation, triggering a feedback loop of rising interest rates and accelerating asset prices that culminates in an endogenous crash.
