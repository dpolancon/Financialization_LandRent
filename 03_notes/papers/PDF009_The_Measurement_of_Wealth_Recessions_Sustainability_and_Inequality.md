## 3. Core thesis

- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF009_Q01` (1, 2, 4, 11): Standard wealth metrics fail to accurately assess economic sustainability because they conflate non-productive asset inflation (like land and capitalized monopoly rents) with true productive capital, while systematically ignoring the destruction of unmeasured human capital.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF009_Q02` (2, 3, 4, 11): Measured wealth and productive capital are fundamentally different aggregates; wealth can increase dramatically due to the capitalization of land and monopoly rents even as the economy's true productive capacity (capital) stagnates or declines.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF009_Q03` (1, 2): Measured wealth can increase dramatically due to rising land values without any corresponding expansion in the economy's actual productive potential, creating a dangerous divergence between financial wealth and true physical capital.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF009_Q04` (1, 2, 3, 4, 11): Real-estate bubbles and the capitalization of exploitative economic rents artificially inflate measured wealth without increasing the economy's actual productive capital, leading to a dangerous mismeasurement of economic sustainability.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF009_Q05` (6, 8, 9, 10): The missing-capital problem highlights that recessions destroy vast amounts of unmeasured human and social capital, causing policymakers who rely on standard metrics to dangerously underestimate the long-term economic damage of austerity policies.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF009_Q06` (1, 2): The literature review should open by distinguishing between measured wealth and productive capital, as treating them as identical obscures the fact that economies can accumulate financial wealth through asset inflation while their true productive capacity diminishes.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF009_Q07` (2, 3, 4, 11): Stiglitz provides critical vocabulary to critique financialization and localization rents by arguing that rising aggregate wealth often reflects the capitalization of exploitative economic rents and localized real estate bubbles into financial assets, rather than genuine productive capital accumulation.
