## 3. Core thesis

- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF001_Q01` (1, 3, 4): The paper explains the disconnection between declining land output and persistently high land values through the inevitable emergence of "land overvaluation," where land prices are driven by its role as a savings vehicle rather than its fundamental productive rents.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF001_Q02` (4, 13): The Land Overvaluation Theorem formally proves that land inevitably becomes overvalued if the elasticity of substitution between land and labor strictly exceeds one ($\sigma > 1$) and labor productivity structurally outpaces land productivity.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF001_Q03` (6, 7, 8, 9): The fundamental value of land is the present discounted value of its rents, and land overvaluation is distinct from a "pure bubble" because land generates a strictly positive intrinsic dividend ($D_t > 0$), avoiding the indeterminacy associated with valueless pure-bubble assets ($D_t = 0$).
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF001_Q04` (1, 2, 3): The theorem is motivated by the empirical paradox that while land's contribution to employment and GDP has drastically fallen over centuries of economic development, real estate remains the overwhelmingly dominant store of household wealth.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF001_Q05` (19, 20): The urban-land application extends the general theorem by proving that land overvaluation inevitably occurs in modern cities if the elasticity of substitution in the intermediate real estate construction sector exceeds one and construction technology improves.
- [PROJECT TRANSLATION] `PDF001_Q06` (4, 8, 15, 18, 20): The main text of the memo should focus on the Land Overvaluation Theorem condition and the core asset pricing definitions ($V_t, B_t$), while relegating the rigorous stochastic discount factor derivations, Markov transition matrices, and nested production functions to footnotes.
- [SOURCE RESULT] `PDF001_Q07` (6): Treating land overvaluation purely through the lens of substitution elasticity and unbalanced technological progress is limited because the theoretical framework explicitly abstracts from credit markets, leverage, and the role of land as collateral.
