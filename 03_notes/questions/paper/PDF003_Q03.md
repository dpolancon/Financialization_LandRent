## NotebookLM question

What is the general-equilibrium crowding-out mechanism from land speculation to lower capital formation?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] In the model, the general-equilibrium crowding-out mechanism operates through the portfolio allocation of entrepreneurs who must divide their finite aggregate savings (wage income) between productive capital investment and land speculation [1, 2]. Because land acts as an alternative, non-produced store of value, any policy that stimulates real estate demand—such as relaxed land-collateral requirements or expansionary monetary policy—drives up land prices and the relative size of land speculation [3, 4]. Consequently, entrepreneurs must divert a larger share of their available savings to cover the down-payments for land purchases [2]. This mechanically leaves fewer funds available to be leveraged for productive capital investment, directly reducing the aggregate capital stock and lowering the long-term endogenous economic growth rate [3, 5].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: When land acts as an alternative store of value, speculative real estate booms divert finite domestic savings away from productive investments, generating a general-equilibrium crowding-out effect that lowers capital formation and long-term economic growth.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Entrepreneurs allocate their finite wage income between purchasing productive capital and buying land.
  - Expansionary credit policies or relaxed real estate collateral requirements increase the speculative demand for and price of land.
  - Entrepreneurs must use a larger portion of their available savings to cover the down-payments for these more expensive land holdings.
  - This portfolio shift mechanically leaves fewer aggregate savings available to finance and leverage productive capital, reducing long-term capital accumulation and economic growth.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Equation (14a) for the growth rate of capital, $1+g_t = \frac{A}{1-\frac{\theta R^c}{1+r_t}}[\eta(1-\alpha) - (1-\frac{\theta^x R_t^x}{1+r_t})\phi_t]$, demonstrating that an increase in relative land speculation ($\phi_t$) directly reduces the economic growth rate.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: credit crowding-out
- **Critical caveat**: Applying this mechanism to Latin American economies requires external verification, as the model structurally assumes a closed economy reliant strictly on domestic life-cycle savings rather than foreign capital inflows, and it abstracts from the region's specific history of concentrated land ownership and extractivism.
