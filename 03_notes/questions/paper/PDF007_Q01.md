## NotebookLM question

How does adding land to the OLG global-dynamics model change the dynamics of capital, wages, output, and land prices?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] Adding land to the baseline OLG model transforms the economy's global dynamics by introducing a non-produced store of value that directly competes with productive capital for finite domestic savings [1-3]. As land prices rise during a speculative boom, they mechanically crowd out real capital accumulation, which consequently lowers future wages and output while driving up the interest rate [2-4]. This higher interest rate temporarily fuels even faster exponential increases in land prices until the asset becomes so overvalued that the trajectory becomes unsustainable, forcing expectations to endogenously turn bearish and triggering a real estate crash [5-7]. Furthermore, the introduction of land expands the mathematical parameter space for instability; it creates "wobbly dynamics" (endogenous, non-converging boom-bust cycles in capital, output, and prices) even under conditions where a landless economy would otherwise settle into a stable, unique full-employment steady state [8-10].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Introducing land as an alternative asset fundamentally alters macroeconomic dynamics by crowding out productive capital, which lowers wages and output while expanding the conditions under which the economy experiences endless, expectation-driven boom-bust cycles.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Young agents allocate their finite life-cycle savings between purchasing productive capital and buying non-produced land.
  - During a land boom, rising land prices absorb a larger share of savings, mechanically crowding out real capital accumulation.
  - The resulting capital shortage depresses wages and aggregate output but increases the return on capital (interest rates).
  - High interest rates accelerate the explosive land boom until prices reach a mathematical upper bound, at which point rational expectations dictate a market crash, restarting the wobbly cycle.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: The savings-investment equation, $k_{t+1} + P_t = s_t(w_t + e)$, demonstrating direct crowding-out; and Proposition 2d, proving the parameter set for wobbly dynamics is strictly larger with land than without it.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: credit crowding-out
- **Critical caveat**: The model relies entirely on a closed-economy framework where land speculation is funded strictly by domestic life-cycle savings without credit frictions; applying this to Latin American land dynamics requires external verification regarding the region's exposure to foreign capital, dollarized real estate, and external sovereign debt.
