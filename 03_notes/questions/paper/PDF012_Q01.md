## NotebookLM question

What empirical objection about stationary housing rent-yield ratios is this short paper answering?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] Toda (2025) directly addresses the empirical objection raised by skeptics who argue that rational land or housing bubbles cannot exist because real-world housing rent yields (the rent-to-price ratio) appear to be stationary. In standard rational bubble models on dividend-paying assets, the presence of a bubble mathematically implies that the dividend yield must vanish over time. Because empirical researchers observe stationary housing rent yields, they conclude that land bubbles are counterfactual. Toda counters this objection by demonstrating that this confusion stems from a measurement error: failing to distinguish between the rent generated by the reproducible housing *structure* and the pure *land* rent. Using an overlapping generations model where the aggregate rent-to-price ratio is strictly constant, Toda proves that a rational land bubble can and inevitably does emerge alongside economic growth, rendering the stationarity of gross housing rent-yields an invalid empirical basis for dismissing land bubbles.

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: The paper refutes the empirical objection that stationary housing rent-yields rule out rational bubbles, demonstrating that land bubbles inevitably emerge even when the aggregate housing rent-to-price ratio is constant.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Empirical skeptics dismiss rational land bubbles because standard theory states a bubble asset's dividend yield must vanish over time, whereas observed housing rent-yields appear stationary.
  - This empirical objection incorrectly conflates the total housing rent (which includes the return on the reproducible housing structure) with the pure dividend of the non-reproducible underlying land.
  - When separating the rent on structures from pure land rents in a growing economy, the total housing rent-yield can remain perfectly constant.
  - Because the pure land dividend yield vanishes relative to the structure, a rational land bubble still inevitably emerges as the economy grows.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Proposition 1 ($r_t h_t / P_t = \beta_h / \beta_o$), proving the aggregate rent-to-price ratio is constant, alongside Proposition 3, which proves the necessity of a land bubble ($\sum_{t=1}^{\infty} e_t^{1/\sigma-1} < \infty$) when the elasticity of substitution between land and structures is $\sigma > 1$.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: critique, land overvaluation
- **Critical caveat**: Assessing the empirical disentanglement of pure land rents from housing structure rents in Latin America requires external verification, as the paper's framework is purely theoretical and abstracts from the region's specific data limitations regarding informal housing structures, volatile urban real estate markets, and missing cadastral data.
