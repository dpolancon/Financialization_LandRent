## NotebookLM question

What is the feedback loop between land prices and capital accumulation?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The feedback loop between land prices and capital accumulation is characterized by a vicious cycle of crowding-out and accelerating asset prices [1]. In the overlapping generations framework, young agents allocate their savings between productive capital and land [2]. When land prices rise, they absorb a larger share of domestic savings, mechanically crowding out productive capital accumulation [3]. This resulting capital shortage drives up the marginal return on capital (the interest rate) while depressing wages [4, 5]. Because the return on land must match the interest rate to satisfy capital arbitrage, the higher interest rate dictates that land prices must increase at an even faster exponential rate, which further exacerbates the crowding out of capital in subsequent periods [1, 5]. This explosive feedback loop continues until land prices reach an unsustainable threshold, inevitably triggering an endogenous market crash [1, 6].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Rising land prices trigger a self-reinforcing feedback loop that crowds out real capital accumulation, driving up interest rates and necessitating even faster, ultimately unsustainable exponential increases in land prices.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Young agents allocate finite life-cycle savings between non-produced land and productive capital.
  - Higher land prices absorb a greater share of these savings, crowding out capital investment.
  - The resulting scarcity of capital lowers wages and raises the interest rate (the marginal return on capital).
  - To satisfy the capital arbitrage equation, higher interest rates force land prices to rise even faster, accelerating the crowding-out cycle until expectations turn bearish and the market crashes.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: The savings/capital accumulation equation (2), $k_{t+1} + P_t = s_t(w_t + e)$, and the capital arbitrage equation (1), $\frac{f_T(k_{t+1})}{P_t} + \frac{P_{t+1}}{P_t} = 1 + r_{t+1}$.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: credit crowding-out
- **Critical caveat**: Applying this feedback loop to Latin American economies requires external verification, as the model structurally assumes a closed economy where land booms are fueled strictly by domestic life-cycle savings, abstracting from the region's reliance on foreign capital flows and external debt.
