## NotebookLM question

What open questions remain for applying the model to fiscal and financialized-land regimes in Latin America?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] Applying this theoretical model to fiscal and financialized-land regimes in Latin America requires external verification, as the provided text does not explicitly mention the region or its specific institutional characteristics. However, inferring from the paper's strict theoretical boundaries, several open questions emerge for the project. The authors construct their framework using a deterministic, closed-economy overlapping-generations model featuring a single safe interest rate and explicitly assume that there is "no aggregate uncertainty" [1, 2]. Because the model's mechanism for infinite debt rollover relies on the government smoothly issuing safe debt to absorb a portion of an efficient land bubble [3], a major open question is how this translates to Latin American economies characterized by severe aggregate uncertainty, high sovereign risk premiums, and foreign-currency-denominated public debt. Furthermore, it remains an open question whether a financialized, highly dollarized Latin American real estate sector could sustain the "Pareto efficient" bubble necessary for indefinite debt rollover when exposed to open-economy shocks like sudden stops in foreign capital flows.

- **Mechanism**:
  - The model demonstrates that infinite debt rollover is possible when a government issues debt to absorb a proportional share of an inevitably emerging, efficient land bubble.

  - Latin American fiscal and financialized-land regimes are defined by high aggregate uncertainty, open-economy capital flows, sovereign risk premiums, and dollarization.
  - Consequently, it remains an open question whether the state can sustainably capture land-bubble rents to roll over debt in a volatile peripheral economy exposed to external shocks.
