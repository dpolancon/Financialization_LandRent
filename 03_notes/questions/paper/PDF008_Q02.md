## NotebookLM question

What is the leverage threshold that separates the fundamental region from the land-bubble region?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The leverage threshold that separates the fundamental region from the land-bubble region is mathematically defined as the critical value ($\overline{\lambda}$) at which the macro-economy's endogenous growth rate transitions from zero to positive growth ($G=1$) [1]. Formally, this threshold is determined by the agents' discount factor, the asymptotic marginal product of capital, and the cross-sectional distribution of entrepreneurial productivity [1]. When leverage is below this critical threshold ($\lambda < \overline{\lambda}$), the positive feedback loop between capital investment and land prices is too weak to sustain endogenous growth; the economy converges to a stationary steady state where it exhibits balanced growth and land prices perfectly reflect their fundamental value (the present value of rents) [2, 3]. Conversely, when leverage is relaxed beyond this critical value ($\lambda > \overline{\lambda}$), the financial accelerator becomes strong enough to trigger unbalanced endogenous growth [2, 3]. In this regime, aggregate capital and aggregate wealth grow rapidly, generating a demand for land as a store of value that causes land prices to grow strictly faster than land rents, thereby creating a rational asset price bubble [2-4].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: The leverage threshold $\overline{\lambda}$ marks the critical tipping point where the financial accelerator becomes strong enough to permanently shift the economy from a stationary, fundamental-value regime to a nonstationary, unbalanced-growth regime characterized by rational land bubbles.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Below the leverage threshold, productive agents cannot borrow enough to continuously expand aggregate capital, leading the economy to settle into a balanced-growth steady state where land prices equal the present value of rents.
  - As leverage is relaxed beyond the critical value $\overline{\lambda}$, productive entrepreneurs can borrow sufficiently against their equity to trigger a powerful financial accelerator.
  - This accelerator forcefully drives capital accumulation and aggregate wealth upwards, breaking the economy's balanced-growth property.
  - Consequently, the economy takes off into endogenous unbalanced growth where the surging demand for land as a store of value forces land prices to grow exponentially faster than land rents, inflating a permanent, rational bubble.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Equation (3.11) defining the threshold: $\overline{\lambda} := \frac{1-\beta}{\beta}\frac{1}{\int_{1/m}^{\infty}(mz-1)d\Phi(z)}$; and Theorem 1 (Land Bubble Characterization).
- **Relevance for the project memo**: land overvaluation, land-price inflation
- **Critical caveat**: Applying this precise leverage threshold to Latin American economies requires external verification, as the threshold is strictly derived assuming domestic wealth accumulation in a single-currency environment, abstracting from the region's historical reliance on external sovereign debt, volatile foreign capital inflows, and dollarized real estate markets.
