## NotebookLM question

What does this baseline paper contribute before land is introduced?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The baseline paper establishes a purely theoretical foundation by demonstrating that complex, unstable macroeconomic fluctuations—termed "wobbly dynamics"—can emerge in a standard, frictionless life-cycle model with production, even before realistic assets like land or credit are introduced [1, 2]. By moving away from the standard representative-agent framework to an overlapping generations model with finite-lived heterogeneous agents, the authors prove that multiple momentary equilibria can arise naturally if the aggregate savings rate decreases as interest rates rise [1, 3]. This non-monotonic savings behavior allows rational expectations to be driven by self-fulfilling "bullish" or "bearish" beliefs, causing the economy to endogenously bounce between economic booms and stagnation traps without ever converging to a unique steady state [4, 5]. The paper thus serves as a proof-of-concept that laissez-faire capitalism is intrinsically unstable, providing the mathematical architecture (such as phase and state transitions) required before subsequent papers layer on the amplifying effects of land speculation and credit cycles [2, 6].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: The baseline paper proves that even without introducing land or credit, a standard overlapping generations economy naturally generates endless, expectation-driven macroeconomic instability ("wobbly dynamics") rather than smooth convergence to a steady state.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Finite-lived heterogeneous agents save for retirement, making aggregate savings dependent on expected future interest rates.
  - If the intertemporal elasticity of substitution is less than unity, a dominant income effect causes savings to fall as interest rates rise, creating a non-monotonic savings curve.
  - This non-monotonicity generates multiple valid market-clearing levels of future capital (multiple momentary equilibria) for a single given current capital stock.
  - Consequently, self-fulfilling beliefs (animal spirits) drive the economy to endlessly jump between unstable booms and stagnation traps, establishing the baseline instability that the introduction of land will later amplify.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Lemma 3 (Sufficient condition for $\Omega^{\prime}(k_{t+1}) < 0$); Proposition 3 (A complete characterization of all possible patterns of wobbly dynamics).
- **Relevance for the project memo**: critique
- **Critical caveat**: The baseline model relies strictly on a closed economy driven by domestic life-cycle savings to generate its wobbly dynamics; applying this purely domestic, expectation-driven instability to Latin America requires external verification regarding the region's exposure to exogenous global financial shocks and foreign capital flows.
