## NotebookLM question

Which parts of this model help answer the project question on land-price inflation and capital formation?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The parts of the model that directly address land-price inflation and capital formation are the savings-investment equation and the capital arbitrage equation within the overlapping generations framework [1, 2]. The savings equation demonstrates a direct crowding-out effect: finite domestic savings are split between purchasing productive capital and buying non-produced land, so an increase in land prices mechanically subtracts from capital accumulation [2]. Consequently, this capital shortage drives up the interest rate [3]. The capital arbitrage equation then dictates that to match this higher interest rate, land prices must inflate at an even faster exponential rate [1, 4]. Together, these components model a vicious feedback loop where land-price inflation increasingly starves the real economy of productive capital until the asset becomes so overvalued that rational expectations force an endogenous market crash [3, 4].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: The model's savings and capital arbitrage equations jointly demonstrate that land-price inflation mechanically crowds out productive capital formation, triggering a feedback loop of rising interest rates and accelerating asset prices that culminates in an endogenous crash.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Young agents allocate finite life-cycle savings between productive capital and land.
  - As land prices inflate, they absorb a larger share of savings, directly crowding out capital formation.
  - Reduced capital formation lowers aggregate wages and raises the marginal return on capital (the interest rate).
  - To satisfy capital arbitrage, the higher interest rate forces land prices to inflate even faster, accelerating the crowding-out cycle until the market endogenously crashes.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: The savings/capital accumulation equation (2), $k_{t+1} + P_t = s_t(w_t + e)$, and the capital arbitrage equation (1), $\frac{f_T(k_{t+1})}{P_t} + \frac{P_{t+1}}{P_t} = 1 + r_{t+1}$.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: land-price inflation, credit crowding-out
- **Critical caveat**: Applying these mechanisms to Latin American economies requires external verification, as the model assumes a closed economy where land booms are fueled strictly by domestic life-cycle savings, abstracting from the region's exposure to external debt and foreign capital inflows.
