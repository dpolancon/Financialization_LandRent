## NotebookLM question

What welfare distribution appears between the generation enjoying the asset boom and later generations facing lower growth?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] A low-interest-rate policy or productivity shock that triggers a real estate boom creates a stark intergenerational welfare trade-off between current asset holders and future generations. The initial generation enjoying the boom—specifically, elderly entrepreneurs who hold land and young entrepreneurs who borrow to invest—experiences an immediate welfare increase due to unexpected capital gains, reduced real debt burdens (if inflation rises), and increased leverage. However, because this speculative boom actively diverts finite savings away from productive capital, it structurally retards long-term productivity and economic growth. Consequently, all agents in subsequent generations—both future savers and future entrepreneurs—suffer from a persistent slowdown in wage growth. The policies and shocks that stimulate temporary asset price booms therefore benefit the initial asset holders and borrowers at the direct expense of the long-term macroeconomic welfare of future generations.

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Speculative asset booms generate a severe intergenerational inequality, enriching the initial generation of asset holders and borrowers while permanently impoverishing future generations through lower wages and stunted economic growth.
- **Mechanism**:
  - An expansionary monetary policy or real estate productivity shock triggers a surge in land prices.
  - This generates immediate, unexpected capital gains for current landholders (elderly entrepreneurs) and advantageous leverage conditions for current borrowers (young entrepreneurs).
  - However, the speculative real estate boom diverts domestic savings away from productive capital investment (a general-equilibrium crowding-out effect).
  - This persistent underinvestment in capital reduces long-term productivity and wage growth, leaving all subsequent generations of workers and entrepreneurs with unambiguously lower welfare.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Proposition 6 (The welfare implications of a low-interest-rate policy) and Proposition 7 (The short- and long-term impact of a real estate boom).
- **Relevance for the project memo**: critique
- **Critical caveat**: The model assumes a closed overlapping-generations economy where intergenerational welfare is strictly mediated by domestic wages and life-cycle savings. Applying these exact distributional outcomes to Latin America requires external verification regarding the region's reliance on foreign capital, external sovereign debt burdens passed to future generations, and historic land concentration.
