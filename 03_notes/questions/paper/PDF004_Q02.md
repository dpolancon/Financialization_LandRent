## NotebookLM question

How do multiple momentary equilibria create many rational-expectations trajectories within bounds?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] In the provided framework, the existence of multiple momentary equilibria—where different savings rates and interest rates can rationally co-exist for a given level of capital—translates into an infinity of possible dynamic trajectories, all fully consistent with rational expectations. Because the economy can suddenly and endogenously switch between a "bullish" high-investment/low-interest-rate equilibrium and a "bearish" low-investment/high-interest-rate equilibrium based entirely on self-fulfilling beliefs, the macroeconomic trajectory "wobbles" without ever converging to a steady state. However, these endless fluctuations remain strictly within calculable bounds. For example, if land prices rise explosively during a bullish phase and approach a critical upper threshold (e.g., $P_2$), the multiplicity of equilibria vanishes; to avoid a mathematically impossible explosive path, the economy must endogenously switch to a bearish, low-return regime (a crash) before exceeding this limit. Thus, the ability to jump between multiple momentary equilibria generates continuous, expectation-driven boom-bust cycles that are permanently contained within structural upper and lower bounds.

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Multiple momentary equilibria allow an economy to endogenously switch between "bullish" and "bearish" states driven by self-fulfilling expectations, generating an infinite number of bounded rational-expectations trajectories (wobbly dynamics) rather than smooth convergence to a unique steady state.
- **Mechanism**:
  - For a given level of capital (and land prices), the non-monotonic savings-investment relationship allows for multiple valid interest rates and savings rates to clear the market.
  - Based purely on shifts in beliefs ("animal spirits"), the economy can dynamically jump back and forth between high-return and low-return momentary equilibria.
  - This continuous switching creates endless, non-converging fluctuations ("wobbles") that remain entirely consistent with rational expectations.
  - These trajectories are strictly bounded because if asset prices approach critical limits (e.g., land prices near $P_2$), the multiplicity disappears; the market is forced into a unique equilibrium that triggers an endogenous crash or reversal to prevent mathematically unsustainable explosive paths.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Equation (2.5b) $\Omega(k_{t+1})=W(k_t)$ establishing the multiplicity condition, and the upper bound $P_2 \equiv \frac{e-(1+\frac{a_1}{a_2}(1-\delta))\frac{\omega_1}{\omega_2}}{(1+\frac{a_1}{a_2}(1-\delta))}$ triggering endogenous crashes.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: critique
- **Critical caveat**: The framework’s generation of bounded trajectories relies strictly on a closed overlapping-generations economy driven purely by domestic life-cycle savings. Applying this closed-system, endogenous-expectation model to Latin American boom-bust cycles requires external verification regarding the region's exposure to exogenous global financial shocks, foreign debt, and foreign exchange constraints.
