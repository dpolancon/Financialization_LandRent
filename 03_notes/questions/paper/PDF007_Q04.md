## NotebookLM question

What critical thresholds force transitions from bullish to bearish expectations or vice versa?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] Transitions from bullish to bearish expectations (and vice versa) are forced by critical price thresholds—specifically an upper bound ($\hat{P}$) and a lower bound ($\underline{P}$)—beyond which the multiplicity of momentary equilibria collapses into a unique equilibrium. During a real estate boom, if land prices exceed the upper threshold $\hat{P}$, the mathematical trajectory would inevitably breach the absolute maximum price ($P_2$) in the following period, forcing prices to explode to infinity. Because an infinitely explosive path violates rational expectations, the market must undergo an endogenous phase transition where bearish expectations (a market crash) become the unique valid momentary equilibrium. Symmetrically, during a bust, if land prices fall below the lower threshold $\underline{P}$, remaining bearish would cause prices to implode below the absolute minimum feasible price ($P_{min}$), eventually becoming zero or negative. To prevent this impossible outcome, the economy must undergo another phase transition where bullish expectations (a land boom) become the unique equilibrium.

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Critical land-price thresholds ($\hat{P}$ and $\underline{P}$) force endogenous phase transitions between bullish and bearish states, ensuring that speculative trajectories remain mathematically bounded and consistent with rational expectations rather than exploding to infinity or imploding to zero.
- **Mechanism**:
  - During a bullish boom, land prices rise exponentially and absorb increasing amounts of capital.
  - If the price crosses the critical upper threshold $\hat{P}$, continuing the boom would push future prices beyond $P_2$, an absolute ceiling where switching to a low-return state is no longer possible.
  - To prevent an impossible, infinitely explosive trajectory, rational expectations force the market to endogenously switch to the unique bearish (low-return) equilibrium, triggering a crash.
  - Conversely, during a bust, if prices fall below the critical lower threshold $\underline{P}$, the market must endogenously switch back to the unique bullish (high-return) equilibrium to prevent prices from imploding into negative values.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: The upper threshold $\hat{P} \equiv \min(\frac{P_2}{\frac{A}{\omega_1}+1-\delta}, P_1)$ and the lower threshold $\underline{P} \equiv \max\{q_l, \frac{P_{min}(D)+D}{1-\delta}\}$.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: land overvaluation, critique
- **Critical caveat**: The model defines these critical expectation thresholds strictly within a closed-economy overlapping-generations framework; applying these exact tipping points to Latin America requires external verification regarding how foreign capital inflows, dollarized real estate, and external debt alter the bounds of rational market trajectories.
