## NotebookLM question

Under what unbalanced-growth conditions can land bubbles and debt rollover emerge?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] Under unbalanced-growth conditions, land bubbles and infinite debt rollover emerge when the economy is characterized by a labor productivity growth rate that exceeds land productivity growth ($G > 1$) and an elasticity of substitution between the two factors that is strictly greater than one ($\sigma > 1$) [1, 2]. In this environment, the asymptotic growth rate of land rents ($G_r = G^{1/\sigma}$) remains positive but is strictly lower than the overall economic growth rate ($G$) [3, 4]. If demographic and endowment factors—such as a low relative income for the old generation—compel the young to save aggressively, the natural interest rate ($R$) is suppressed below the rent growth rate, creating the condition $R < G_r < G$ [5-7]. Under these specific conditions, standard asset pricing fundamentals fail to clear the market, meaning an asymptotically growing land price bubble inevitably emerges to absorb the excess savings, thereby achieving Pareto efficiency [5, 8]. Because this necessary land bubble grows exactly at the rate of interest, the government can sustainably siphon off a proportional share of the bubble by issuing public debt, making infinite debt rollover feasible despite the economy remaining Pareto efficient [9, 10].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Land bubbles and infinite debt rollover inevitably emerge under unbalanced growth when the elasticity of substitution between labor and land exceeds one ($\sigma > 1$), labor productivity outpaces land productivity, and high savings motives drive the natural interest rate below the dividend growth rate.
- **Mechanism**:
  - The economy undergoes unbalanced growth where labor productivity grows faster than fixed land productivity ($G > 1$), and the two inputs are highly substitutable ($\sigma > 1$).
  - This structural imbalance causes land rents to grow asymptotically at a rate ($G^{1/\sigma}$) that is slower than overall output growth ($G$).
  - If the old generation's relative income is low, the young save heavily, pushing the natural interest rate ($R$) below the land rent growth rate.
  - To prevent infinite fundamental land prices, an efficient land bubble must inevitably emerge; the fiscal authority can then sustain infinite debt rollover by issuing bonds that absorb a fraction of this growing bubble.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Theorem 1 ($R < G_r < G$) and Proposition 3.1 ($R < G^{1/\sigma}$ with $G>1, \sigma>1$), proving that under these conditions all equilibria are asymptotically bubbly, Pareto efficient, and permit infinite debt rollover.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: debt rollover, land overvaluation
- **Critical caveat**: Applying these precise unbalanced-growth prerequisites to Latin America requires external verification, as the theoretical model relies on formalized CES production mechanics and abstracts from the region's structural heterogeneity, informal labor dynamics, and commodity-export-driven land valuations.
