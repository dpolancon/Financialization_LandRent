## NotebookLM question

Why does the paper argue that unbalanced growth is necessary for debt rollover with land?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The paper argues that unbalanced growth is necessary for infinite debt rollover in an economy with land because balanced growth mathematically guarantees that the safe interest rate exceeds the economic growth rate ($R > G$), which structurally precludes sustainable Ponzi schemes [1, 2]. Under a balanced growth path—such as when the elasticity of substitution between land and labor is exactly one or when their productivities grow at the same rate—land rents, land prices, and aggregate output all grow at the exact same rate [3]. Because land provides a positive rental dividend in addition to its capital appreciation, its total rate of return must strictly exceed the economic growth rate; standard no-arbitrage conditions thus force the safe interest rate to exceed the growth rate as well ($R > G$) [2, 4]. If a government attempts to roll over debt infinitely in this $R > G$ environment, the debt will grow faster than the economy itself, making the scheme unsustainable [3, 5]. Therefore, to suppress the interest rate sufficiently to allow for the inevitable emergence of a sustainable land bubble and infinite debt rollover, the economy must experience unbalanced growth, where labor productivity outpaces land productivity ($G > 1$) and the factors are highly substitutable ($\sigma > 1$) [3, 6].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Unbalanced growth is theoretically necessary for infinite debt rollover in an economy with land because balanced growth structurally forces the interest rate above the economic growth rate ($R > G$), rendering any Ponzi scheme unsustainable.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Under balanced growth, fundamental land rents, land prices, and overall economic output grow at identical rates.
  - Because land yields a positive intrinsic dividend, its total return strictly exceeds the growth rate of the economy.
  - No-arbitrage conditions dictate that the safe interest rate on government debt must equal the return on land, mathematically ensuring $R > G$.
  - With $R > G$, any infinitely rolled-over public debt would grow faster than the economy, forcing unsustainability; thus, unbalanced growth is required to suppress the natural interest rate and enable sustainable debt rollover.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Proposition 3.3, which proves that under balanced growth ($G=1$ or $\sigma=1$), $R > G$ necessarily emerges and infinite debt rollover is impossible.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: debt rollover
- **Critical caveat**: Applying the necessity of unbalanced growth for debt rollover to Latin America requires external verification, as the region's sovereign debt sustainability is frequently dictated by external shocks, foreign-currency denomination, and sudden stops in capital flows, rather than the deterministic, closed-economy productivity differentials modeled here.
