## NotebookLM question

What assumptions produce multiplicity of momentary equilibria in the life-cycle model?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The multiplicity of momentary equilibria in this life-cycle model is produced by two structural assumptions regarding heterogeneity and elasticities. First, the model requires **finite-lived heterogeneous agents**, implemented via an overlapping generations framework, because this heterogeneity ensures that savings depend critically on the expected rate of return over the life-cycle [1]. Second, it requires specific parametric assumptions about preferences and technology: **individuals must reduce their savings when the expected interest rate increases**, which occurs when the income effect dominates the substitution effect (i.e., the intertemporal elasticity of substitution $\theta < 1$) [2, 3]. Furthermore, the elasticity of substitution between capital and labor must also be less than unity ($\sigma < 1$) [4, 5]. When these elasticities are sufficiently small (specifically, **$\sigma < 1 - \theta$**) and the aggregate productivity parameter is large enough, the economy's savings-investment mapping becomes non-monotonic [5, 6]. This non-monotonicity is the mathematical catalyst that allows multiple valid levels of future capital investment—and thus multiple clearing interest rates—to coexist for a single given level of current capital [4].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Multiplicity of momentary equilibria arises when an overlapping generations economy features strong gross complementarity in both consumption (low intertemporal elasticity of substitution) and production (low elasticity of substitution between capital and labor), causing the aggregate savings curve to become non-monotonic with respect to interest rates.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Finite-lived heterogeneous agents save for retirement, making their savings rate dependent on expected future interest rates.
  - Because the intertemporal elasticity of substitution is less than unity ($\theta < 1$), higher expected interest rates induce a dominant income effect, causing agents to rationally reduce their savings.
  - Coupled with a low elasticity of substitution between capital and labor ($\sigma < 1$), this creates a non-monotonic (backward-bending) mapping between future capital and current capital.
  - Consequently, for a given initial capital stock, multiple combinations of savings and expected returns (e.g., a "bullish" high-investment/low-return state or a "bearish" low-investment/high-return state) can clear the market, meaning the outcome is driven entirely by self-fulfilling beliefs.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Lemma 3 (Sufficient condition for $\Omega^{\prime}(k_{t+1}) < 0$, requiring $\sigma < 1 - \theta$ and sufficiently large productivity $A$) and Equation (A) $\Omega(k_{t+1}) = W(k_t)$.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: critique
- **Critical caveat**: The model's generation of multiple equilibria strictly assumes a closed economy driven by domestic life-cycle savings, completely abstracting from money, land, and credit; applying these specific elasticity-driven wobbly dynamics to Latin America requires external verification regarding the region's exposure to international capital flows and foreign exchange constraints.
