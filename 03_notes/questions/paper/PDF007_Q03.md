## NotebookLM question

How do land booms become rational-expectations trajectories rather than irrational bubbles?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] Land booms become rational-expectations trajectories, rather than irrational bubbles, because the underlying overlapping-generations framework features a multiplicity of momentary equilibria that allows seemingly explosive price paths to endogenously crash before they become truly impossible [1, 2]. In standard rational-expectations models, explosive bubble paths are ruled out by backward induction because they would require prices to rise to infinity; however, in this wobbly economy, agents rationally know that the land price is bounded by a critical upper threshold ($\hat{P}$ or $P_2$) [3, 4]. So long as expectations endogenously turn "bearish" and the economy switches to a low-return equilibrium before this maximum threshold is breached, the subsequent crash in land prices satisfies the capital arbitrage equation perfectly [4, 5]. Thus, agents buy overvalued land not out of irrational exuberance, but because the rational expectation of an endogenous future market switch allows them to dynamically clear the market without violating the mathematical bounds of a sustainable trajectory [3, 6].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: Seemingly explosive land booms are perfectly consistent with rational expectations because the multiplicity of momentary equilibria guarantees that prices will endogenously crash before reaching an impossible, infinitely explosive threshold.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Standard macroeconomic models rule out explosive asset paths because an asset price rising forever violates rational bounds (the saddle point property).
  - In this overlapping-generations economy, multiple momentary equilibria exist, allowing both high-return (bullish/capital scarcity) and low-return (bearish/capital surplus) states.
  - During a land boom, prices rise exponentially, fully satisfying the capital arbitrage equation in the high-return state.
  - Before the price reaches a critical upper bound where it would have to explode infinitely, expectations endogenously shift to the low-return state, triggering a rational crash that keeps the trajectory mathematically bounded.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: The capital arbitrage equation $\frac{f_T(k_{t+1})}{P_t} + \frac{P_{t+1}}{P_t} = 1 + r_{t+1}$, which holds across the phase transition from the explosive boom into the endogenous crash, bounded by the critical threshold $\hat{P}$.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: critique, land overvaluation
- **Critical caveat**: The model asserts these speculative booms are rational strictly within the parameters of closed-economy domestic life-cycle savings, so validating this mechanism for Latin America requires assessing how external debt and foreign capital flows alter the boundaries and rational expectations of endogenous crashes.
