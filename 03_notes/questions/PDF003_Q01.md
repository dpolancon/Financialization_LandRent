## NotebookLM question

What are the main empirical facts on sectoral credit expansion that motivate the model?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The model is motivated by empirical evidence demonstrating that rapid aggregate credit expansions systematically predict economic growth slowdowns, primarily because credit flows disproportionately into real estate and crowds out productive investment [1]. Using historical data on sectoral credit distribution across 117 countries, researchers show that credit expansions directed to construction and real estate systematically predict subsequent productivity and economic slowdowns, whereas credit flowing to the manufacturing sector is associated with higher productivity and stronger macroeconomic performance [1]. Furthermore, firm-level data indicates that housing price booms directly crowd out commercial investment by decreasing bank lending to credit-constrained firms [2]. Finally, as economies develop, they structurally shift toward higher shares of real estate credit and an increased reliance on high-priced real estate collateral, which persistently slows long-term productivity and growth rates [1].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: The macroeconomic impact of a credit boom depends strictly on its sectoral composition, as credit expansions directed toward real estate systematically crowd out productive commercial investment and retard long-term economic growth.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Financial liberalization and expansionary monetary policy lead to rapid aggregate credit expansions.
  - This credit disproportionately flows into the real estate sector and increases reliance on real estate collateral.
  - The resulting real estate and housing price booms cause banks to decrease lending to productive, credit-constrained commercial firms (a crowding-out effect).
  - Consequently, the lack of productive capital investment leads to long-term productivity and economic growth slowdowns.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Not applicable.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: credit crowding-out
- **Critical caveat**: The source does not specifically address Latin American economies; applying these empirical patterns to Latin American debates requires external verification regarding the region's specific sectoral credit composition, reliance on primary exports, and historical land concentration.
