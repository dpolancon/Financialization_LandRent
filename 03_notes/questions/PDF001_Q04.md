## NotebookLM question

Which empirical facts and figures motivate the theorem, including the decline of land-intensive sectors and the wealth share of real estate?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The Land Overvaluation Theorem is motivated by a stark macroeconomic divergence between the declining productive role of land and its persistently dominant role in household wealth. Empirically, the authors highlight that the employment share of land-intensive sectors (agriculture, mining, and construction) has significantly declined in the United States over the past three centuries as per capita GDP has grown, a structural transformation also visible in cross-sectional data from 173 countries in 2023. Conversely, data from 29 OECD countries demonstrate that real estate—comprising primary residences and secondary properties—still constitutes over 50% of gross household wealth in 27 of those nations. The authors formulate their theorem to mathematically reconcile this apparent empirical disconnection, showing how unbalanced productivity growth inherently drives land prices up as a store of value even as land's share of productive output shrinks.

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: The theorem is motivated by the empirical paradox that while land's contribution to employment and GDP has drastically fallen over centuries of economic development, real estate remains the overwhelmingly dominant store of household wealth.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Historical data show a continuous decline in the employment and GDP shares of land-intensive sectors (agriculture, mining, construction) as economies develop and per capita incomes rise.
  - Despite its declining productive importance, land possesses unique characteristics (durability, intrinsic value, immobility) that make it an exceptionally stable savings vehicle.
  - Aggregate household wealth consequently remains heavily concentrated in real estate, exceeding 50% of total wealth in almost all OECD nations.
  - This empirical disconnection explicitly motivates the authors to build a model demonstrating how unbalanced growth causes land values to decouple from their diminishing fundamental rents.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Figure 1 (US employment share of agriculture/land-intensive sectors), Figure 2 (Cross-country GDP/employment share of agriculture), and Figure 3 (Composition of gross household wealth in OECD countries).
- **Relevance for the project memo**: land overvaluation
- **Critical caveat**: Because these motivating empirical facts rely primarily on US historical time-series and OECD wealth compositions, confirming the exact nature of this "disconnection" in Latin America requires external verification, as the region may feature distinct wealth distributions, pervasive informal housing, and a persistently high economic reliance on primary, land-intensive commodity exports.
