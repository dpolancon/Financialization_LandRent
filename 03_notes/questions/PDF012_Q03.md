## NotebookLM question

How can a land bubble exist despite a non-vanishing or constant housing rent yield?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] A land bubble can exist alongside a constant or non-vanishing housing rent yield because the aggregate housing rent-to-price ratio conflates two distinct components: the rent accruing to the reproducible housing structure and the pure dividend of the underlying land [1, 2]. Toda demonstrates that in a growing economy where housing is produced using both construction materials and land, the total housing rent yield can remain perfectly stationary (and even mathematically constant) because it is dominated by the returns on the reproducible housing structure [2, 3]. However, when the pure land rent is explicitly separated from the overall structure's rent, its specific dividend yield asymptotically vanishes [4]. Because standard economic theory only requires the pure land dividend yield to vanish to satisfy the mathematical conditions for a rational bubble, a land bubble inevitably emerges alongside economic growth, entirely masked by the empirical stationarity of the overall housing rent yield [1, 5].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: A land bubble can exist despite a constant overall housing rent yield because the total housing rent is dominated by the return on reproducible structures; once separated, the underlying pure land dividend yield mathematically vanishes, allowing the bubble to form.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Empirical skeptics observe a stationary or constant aggregate housing rent-to-price ratio and falsely conclude rational bubbles cannot exist, as standard theory requires a bubble asset's dividend yield to vanish over time.
  - However, the aggregate housing rent compensates both the reproducible housing structure and the non-reproducible underlying land.
  - In a growing economy, the total housing rent yield can remain constant because it mostly reflects the rent on the expanding reproducible structure.
  - Simultaneously, the pure land dividend yield actually vanishes relative to the structure's value, satisfying the necessary mathematical condition that allows a rational land bubble to inevitably emerge.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Proposition 1 ($r_1 h_1 / P_1 = \beta_h / \beta_o$), proving the aggregate housing rent-to-price ratio is constant, and Proposition 3, proving the necessity of a land bubble ($\sum_{t=1}^{\infty} e_t^{1/\sigma-1} < \infty$) when the elasticity of substitution between land and structures is $\sigma > 1$.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: land overvaluation, critique
- **Critical caveat**: Empirically executing the precise disaggregation of pure land rents from structural rents in Latin America requires external verification, as the theoretical model assumes perfectly measured formal production functions and abstracts from the region's specific realities of informal, self-built housing and missing or outdated cadastral property valuations.
