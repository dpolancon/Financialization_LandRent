## NotebookLM question

How does the model define a phase transition from balanced growth to unbalanced bubbly growth?

## NotebookLM answer

> [NOTEBOOKLM] The model defines a phase transition as an abrupt, qualitative shift in macroeconomic dynamics—from a stationary regime of balanced growth to a nonstationary regime of unbalanced, bubbly growth—triggered when financial leverage is relaxed beyond a critical threshold [1, 2]. When leverage is below this threshold, the financial accelerator is too weak to sustain endless expansion, causing the economy to converge to a steady state where aggregate capital, land prices, and land rents all grow at the same rate, ensuring land prices strictly reflect their fundamental value [1]. However, once leverage exceeds the critical value, the positive feedback loop between capital investment and land prices becomes powerful enough that the economy suddenly loses its balanced-growth property and takes off into endogenous growth [1]. In this unbalanced growth regime, land prices grow at the same rapid rate as the overall economy (driven by demand for land as a store of wealth), while land rents grow at a slower rate (driven by production demand), causing the price-rent ratio to diverge to infinity and inflating a rational asset price bubble [1, 2].

## Distilled note

- **Core claim**: A phase transition to unbalanced bubbly growth occurs when financial leverage exceeds a critical threshold, strengthening the financial accelerator enough to permanently shift the economy from stationary balanced growth into endogenous unbalanced growth where land prices exponentially outpace rents.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Below the critical leverage threshold, the economy converges to a balanced-growth steady state where land prices and rents grow at the same rate.
  - When leverage exceeds the critical value, the positive feedback loop between capital investment and land wealth strengthens sufficiently to trigger endogenous economic growth.
  - Aggregate capital and overall wealth begin to grow rapidly, fueling sustained demand for land as a store of value.
  - Consequently, land prices grow strictly faster than the marginal product of land (rents), generating an explosive, non-stationary price-rent ratio and a permanent rational land bubble.
- **Formal object/result, if any**: Theorem 1 (Land Bubble Characterization), which formally establishes the phase transition at the leverage threshold $\overline{\lambda} := \frac{1-\beta}{\beta}\frac{1}{\int_{1/m}^{\infty}(mz-1)d\Phi(z)}$.
- **Relevance for the project memo**: land overvaluation, land-price inflation
- **Critical caveat**: Assessing this exact phase-transition mechanism in Latin American economies requires external verification, as the model's financial accelerator operates through uniform domestic leverage and wealth accumulation, abstracting from the region's reliance on foreign capital inflows, dollarized asset markets, and structural external debt constraints.
